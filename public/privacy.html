Privacy Policy

Canonical Shelf is designed to keep core study activity local while being explicit about the limited data that is processed when you choose accounts, synchronization, feedback, or cloud Theologian features.

Effective September 22, 2026

1. Scope and principles

This policy describes Canonical Shelf's handling of information when you use the website, progressive web app, account and synchronization features, feedback and review tools, and Theologian. Canonical Shelf follows three product principles: collect only what a feature needs, keep private study writing local unless you deliberately enable a feature that transfers it, and do not use learning activity to infer what you personally believe.

Theological questions, pastoral conversations, and other content you choose to enter may reveal religious beliefs or other sensitive information. Canonical Shelf treats that content as sensitive and does not use it for advertising, sale, behavioral profiling, or inferred-belief scoring.

2. Information that normally remains on your device

Guest use is local-first. Depending on the feature you use, your browser may store course progress, review state, recent study activity, appearance preferences, Journal writing, the current Theologian transcript, offline assets, an unsent feedback queue, and a random feedback-routing identifier. This information remains on your device unless you deliberately use account synchronization, submit feedback, or send a question to the cloud Theologian.

The current Theologian transcript is stored in the browser so the visible conversation can survive route changes and reloads. Canonical Shelf's application does not persist that chat transcript to D1, KV, Durable Objects, account synchronization, Journal, or Feedback merely because you used Theologian.

3. Accounts and synchronization

If you create or use an account, Canonical Shelf processes the account, authentication, passkey/session, and synchronization information required to provide that feature. Account synchronization is optional. When enabled, selected learner state is stored in Canonical Shelf's Cloudflare D1 database so it can be restored or synchronized.

Deleting an account is designed to delete associated Canonical Shelf learner synchronization state, subject to limited records that must temporarily remain for security, abuse prevention, dispute resolution, or legal obligations.

4. Theologian and cloud AI processing

When you send a question to the cloud Theologian, Canonical Shelf transmits the current question, a bounded recent conversation excerpt, the current route/activity, and an allowlisted study-state summary needed to make the response relevant. Journal text, lesson notes, optional reflection writing, account/profile identifiers, feedback content, and inferred theological beliefs are excluded from this context.

Theologian cloud inference is provided through Cloudflare Workers AI. Cloudflare states that Workers AI inputs and outputs are Customer Content and are not used to train models or improve Cloudflare or third-party services without explicit consent. Canonical Shelf does not intentionally write ordinary Theologian prompts or responses to server-side application storage. A response may be stored only if you deliberately submit it for feedback/review or another feature explicitly tells you it will be saved.

5. Feedback, reviews, and pseudonymous reply routing

You may submit feedback with no account and no contact information. Canonical Shelf creates a high-entropy random identifier in that browser so a reviewer response can return to the same browser. The reusable identifier remains in your browser; Canonical Shelf stores only a one-way SHA-256 routing key with the feedback record. It is a pseudonymous browser link, not proof of a person's identity.

A Theologian response review may include the immediately relevant learner question, the exact Theologian answer, visible evidence metadata, route, response mode/model, policy version, validation status, an optional review reason, and any explanation you choose to provide. It does not automatically include Journal writing, private reflections, profile/account information, inferred beliefs, or unrelated chat history.

Canonical Shelf does not use or persist an IP address as the identifier for feedback, review routing, or crisis handling. If infrastructure providers process network addresses as part of ordinary network/security operations, those provider-level logs are not used by Canonical Shelf to build a learner profile.

6. Crisis and pastoral conversations

If language indicates possible suicide or self-harm risk, a deterministic crisis-safety layer may replace ordinary Theologian generation with a safety-oriented response. Canonical Shelf does not silently contact police, emergency services, clergy, family, or other third parties; does not create a persistent diagnosis or permanent suicidal-risk label; and does not automatically convert a crisis conversation into feedback. The local chat transcript may remain in your browser until you start a new chat or clear browser data.

7. Service providers and disclosures

Canonical Shelf uses Cloudflare infrastructure, including Workers, D1, static asset delivery, and Workers AI. Information is disclosed to service providers only as needed to operate, secure, troubleshoot, or provide the feature you requested. Canonical Shelf does not sell personal data, does not use personal data for targeted advertising, and currently does not run advertising pixels or behavioral analytics trackers.

Information may also be disclosed when reasonably necessary to comply with applicable law, protect the security of the service, investigate abuse, or protect the rights and safety of users or others. Canonical Shelf does not promise that it can dispatch emergency assistance from a Theologian conversation.

8. Cookies and similar browser storage

Canonical Shelf uses first-party authentication/security session cookies when needed for account features and first-party browser storage for requested product functions such as local learning state, appearance, offline operation, Theologian conversation continuity, and anonymous feedback reply routing. Canonical Shelf currently does not use advertising cookies or nonessential behavioral analytics cookies.

Because the current browser storage is used for requested functionality, security, or local-first operation rather than optional tracking, Canonical Shelf does not present a generic “accept all cookies” banner. If nonessential analytics, advertising, or tracking technologies are introduced, they must be disclosed and, where required, disabled until valid consent is obtained. See Cookies & Local Storage for the current inventory and controls.

9. Retention

Retention varies by data type. Local data remains until you clear it, use a product control that removes it, or browser/device storage removes it. Server-side account, synchronization, feedback, and review data follow the periods described in the Data Retention Policy. Canonical Shelf aims to remove data when it is no longer reasonably needed for the feature, security, dispute handling, or applicable legal requirements.

10. Your choices and requests

Depending on your location and applicable law, you may have rights to access, correct, obtain, delete, or restrict certain personal data and to appeal certain privacy-request decisions. Canonical Shelf intends to provide these controls even where a particular statutory right does not apply. You can also clear local browser data, start a new Theologian chat, forget the anonymous feedback link on the current browser, disable account sync, or delete your account where those controls are available.

To make a privacy or data request, use the Canonical Shelf Feedback tool and choose Privacy or data request. Do not include sensitive information that is unnecessary to identify the records or request.

11. Children

Canonical Shelf is designed primarily for adult independent learners and is not directed to children under 13. If Canonical Shelf learns that personal information from a child was collected in a manner that requires parental consent and that consent was not obtained, it will take reasonable steps to delete the information.

12. Changes to this policy

Material changes should be reflected by updating the effective date and, when appropriate, presenting a visible notice in the product. A change to optional tracking, data sale, targeted advertising, Theologian storage, or another materially different use of sensitive information should not be introduced solely through silent policy text.
