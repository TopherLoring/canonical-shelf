Theologian safety

Pastoral support with human safety first.

Theologian can provide biblical, theological, spiritual, and pastoral-style guidance. When credible suicide or self-harm risk appears, immediate safety takes precedence over ordinary theological instruction.

Immediate crisis support

If you are in immediate physical danger, an attempt is underway, you have already seriously harmed yourself, or you may have taken an overdose, call 911 or the appropriate local emergency service or go to the nearest emergency department now.

In the United States, you can call or text 988 to reach the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Canonical Shelf is not an emergency-dispatch service and cannot guarantee that it can identify or respond to every emergency.

What crisis mode does

A deterministic safety layer checks for direct first-person indicators of possible suicide or self-harm before ordinary AI generation. When it activates, Theologian prioritizes 988/emergency guidance, asks plainly about immediate danger, encourages connection with a trusted person, and may recommend creating distance from weapons, medications, or other means of self-harm when relevant.

Crisis mode is intentionally conservative but cannot perfectly distinguish every metaphor, quotation, third-person discussion, or emergency. A general theological question about suicide should not be treated as proof that the learner is suicidal.

Pastoral reassurance

Canonical Shelf's crisis policy explicitly rejects shame-based spiritual intervention. Doubt, depression, despair, suicidal thoughts, and self-harm do not place a learner beyond God's love, grace, presence, or power. A learner does not need to prove sufficient faith before deserving safety, care, or help.

Theologian may encourage a learner to ask God for comfort, healing, courage, hope, strength, and perseverance through a difficult period. It may recommend contacting a trusted pastor, chaplain, clergy member, spiritual director, or another trusted person of shared faith—especially someone who can remain physically present.

Prayer

Theologian may offer to pray with the learner. If the learner asks for prayer, it can provide a brief prayer for safety, comfort, healing, courage, wise human help, strength, hope, and perseverance. Prayer must not replace or delay 988, emergency medical care, emergency services, or other appropriate human support. After prayer, Theologian returns to the safety check rather than treating prayer as resolution of the crisis.

What Theologian must not do

Theologian must not use fear of hell, divine punishment, shame, disappointment from God, or threats about salvation as crisis intervention. It must not tell a learner that stronger faith or prayer alone should resolve suicidality, self-harm, depression, or another emergency, and it must not interpret those experiences as proof that the learner lacks faith or has been abandoned by God.

Privacy in crisis mode

Canonical Shelf does not use or retain IP addresses as crisis identities, does not silently contact law enforcement, clergy, family, or other third parties, does not automatically turn a crisis conversation into feedback, and does not create a permanent mental-health diagnosis or suicidal-risk label from a Theologian conversation.

The local Theologian transcript may remain in the browser until the learner starts a new chat or clears site data. If the learner deliberately submits a crisis response for feedback/review, only the bounded review context disclosed by the Feedback tool is sent.

Learner agency remains intact

Learner agency is a hard requirement. The learner remains the decision-maker. Crisis mode can strongly recommend urgent human help when safety is at risk, but it does not use theological pressure or inferred beliefs to force doctrinal agreement.
