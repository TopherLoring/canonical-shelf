About Canonical Shelf

A Bible-learning environment, reference library, and study tool.

Canonical Shelf combines guided courses, the biblical text, a visual canonical shelf, book profiles, curated Topics, active Practice, and deeper study tools. The product is built for an adult learner who may begin with little biblical background but should not be limited to shallow explanations.

The five primary destinations remain Home, Course, Bible, Topics, and Practice. Institutional disclosures live here rather than competing with those learning and study modes.

How Canonical Shelf approaches Scripture

Text first. Context visible. Interpretation labeled.

Canonical Shelf treats Scripture as inspired and authoritative Christian Scripture while reading passages in literary, historical, cultural, linguistic, and canonical context. It distinguishes what a text says from later assumptions placed on it and keeps biblical text, historical evidence, translation, interpretation, reception, doctrine, and application visibly distinct.

Where Christians or scholars responsibly disagree, Canonical Shelf identifies the disagreement and the evidence behind the principal readings. When Canonical Shelf holds a position on a genuinely disputed question, it names that position, bounds it by the Statement of Faith, and represents interpretations it disagrees with accurately enough for the learner to weigh them. Agreement is never required to use Canonical Shelf; learning activities assess understanding and reasoning, not theological assent, and Canonical Shelf does not use learning or reflection data to infer what a learner believes.

Sources & methodology

Claims should be traceable.

Course and reference content combine primary biblical readings with historical, literary, linguistic, archaeological, cultural, and theological context where relevant. Guided learning adds explicit objectives, plain-language explanations, vocabulary, reflection, and mastery checks; deeper study surfaces preserve source trails, competing readings, and supporting evidence.

Scholarly claims should be traceable, uncertainty should be visible, and contested reconstructions should not be presented as settled fact. Progressive disclosure keeps the main lesson focused while drawers, Topics, Bible tools, and deeper study surfaces make additional background, evidence, interpretation, and sources available when they are useful.

Translation information

Translation is part of interpretation.

The complete local Bible reader currently ships with the Berean Standard Bible corpus. Additional translation wording is shown only where Canonical Shelf has the corresponding text and permission to distribute it; a translation selector must not imply that a complete corpus exists when it does not. Translation controls affect Scripture display, not the substance of the curriculum.

Lessons may compare English translations, manuscript evidence, idioms, semantic ranges, or textual variants when those differences matter to understanding. Translation choices, source-text questions, and later interpretive conclusions are treated as related but distinct issues.

Accessibility

Rich interaction needs an equivalent path.

Canonical Shelf is designed for keyboard navigation, visible focus, screen readers, touch, contrast, zoom and reflow, reduced motion, forced colors, and accessible alternatives to drag or spatial manipulation. Accessibility is treated as a floor for the experience, not a reason to reduce the visual or interactive quality of the default product. Human assistive-technology and device testing remains part of release review.

Privacy

Local-first by default.

Guest and offline use are first-class, and no account is required for the core learning experience. Learning progress, review state, notes, recent activity, and journal writing are designed to remain local on the device unless you deliberately export them or enable optional account sync and backup.

Personal reflection is not scored for theological agreement, and Canonical Shelf does not record or infer theological assent from learning activity. Account and synchronization features are optional additions to the learning experience rather than prerequisites for reading, studying, practicing, or completing courses.
