Data governance

Data Retention Policy

Canonical Shelf keeps information only for as long as the product, learner, security, or legal purpose reasonably requires.

Effective September 22, 2026

Browser-local information

Learning progress, review state, appearance settings, Journal writing, and related local study state: retained on the device until you clear it, reset the corresponding feature, delete site data, or the browser/device removes it.

Theologian transcript: retained in the current browser until you start a new chat or clear site data. The application does not ordinarily persist the transcript server-side.

Anonymous feedback routing identifier: retained in the browser until you choose “Forget this browser's feedback link,” clear site data, or browser storage is removed. Losing it prevents that browser from retrieving later anonymous reviewer responses.

Offline feedback queue: retained locally until delivery succeeds or local site data is cleared. The queue is bounded to the most recent 50 pending submissions.

Offline/PWA assets: retained in browser cache storage until replaced by a new release, cleared by the browser/user, or invalidated by Canonical Shelf's service worker.

Accounts and synchronized learner state

Account and synchronized learner records are retained while the account remains active and the information is needed to provide synchronization. Account deletion is intended to remove associated Canonical Shelf learner state. Limited security, authentication, backup, or dispute records may remain temporarily where necessary to complete deletion, prevent abuse, restore service integrity, or satisfy applicable legal obligations.

Feedback and response reviews

Open or unresolved feedback/reviews: retained for up to 24 months after submission or last meaningful activity so the issue can be investigated and answered.

Responded/resolved feedback/reviews: retained for up to 12 months after the reviewer response or resolution so the learner can receive the response and Canonical Shelf can verify whether a correction worked.

Optional contact information: removed or anonymized no later than 90 days after a feedback/review thread is responded to or resolved unless the learner asks for continued contact or a longer period is needed for a specific dispute or legal obligation.

Pseudonymous browser routing key: stored only as a one-way hash and retained with the associated feedback record. It is removed when the feedback record reaches the end of its retention period.

Theologian review context: follows the same retention period as the feedback/review record to which it belongs.

Theologian cloud processing

Canonical Shelf does not intentionally persist ordinary cloud Theologian prompts and responses in application databases. A bounded conversation remains in the browser, and prompts/outputs are processed by Cloudflare Workers AI to generate the requested response. If you deliberately submit a Theologian response for review, the bounded review snapshot follows the feedback retention schedule above.

Crisis conversations

Canonical Shelf does not create a separate long-term mental-health record, diagnosis, or permanent crisis-risk label. Crisis conversation text follows the same local Theologian transcript behavior unless the learner deliberately submits part of it as feedback. Canonical Shelf does not persist IP addresses as crisis identifiers.

Operational and security logs

Cloudflare infrastructure may generate short-lived invocation, security, and error logs required to operate and protect the service. Canonical Shelf does not treat these logs as learner profiles and does not intentionally copy network addresses into long-term feedback or Theologian records. Provider-controlled log retention follows the applicable Cloudflare configuration and service limits.

Deletion, legal holds, and backups

A valid deletion request may cause records to be removed earlier than the periods above. Canonical Shelf may retain narrowly limited information longer when reasonably required for security, fraud/abuse prevention, dispute resolution, legal claims, or compliance with law. Deleted data may remain temporarily in provider backups or recovery systems until those systems cycle normally, but it should not be restored into active use except for legitimate recovery purposes.

Enforcement

Retention periods should be implemented in product/database cleanup logic rather than existing only in this document. Material changes to these periods require corresponding implementation and policy updates.
