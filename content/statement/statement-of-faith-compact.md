# Statement of Faith

**Canonical Shelf / TopherLoring Ministries**

> **You are not asked to agree with this Statement of Faith to use
> Canonical Shelf.** It publishes the convictions that bound Canonical
> Shelf's own doctrinal claims. Learning activities assess
> understanding, evidence, interpretation, and reasoning---not
> theological assent.

## God

We believe in one God, the loving Creator and source of all existence.
While all creation depends upon God for its existence, created beings
possess genuine agency. This does not mean that God determines every
choice or directly causes every action.

We believe God is Love. Love is not merely something God expresses, but
is fundamental to God's nature and the driving purpose of creation.
Because God is Love, God cannot ultimately act contrary to Love or
permanently abandon what God loves.

We believe in one God with one divine consciousness. Scripture describes
God through the language of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, but we do not
understand these terms as three separate divine consciousnesses or as
literal divine genealogy. The language of Father and Son expresses
relationship in terms human beings can understand.

God may make God's presence physically manifest in whatever forms God
chooses without being confined to a single physical form or location.
The absence of a physical manifestation does not mean the absence of
God.

## Jesus Christ

We believe the divine reality embodied as Jesus did not begin with his
human life. It existed eternally within God and participated in
creation. Scripture describes this preexistent reality as the Word and
the Son, while the precise nature of its relationship within the one God
remains beyond what we can fully understand or prove.

We believe God became physically embodied in Jesus, who was fully divine
and fully human without encompassing the entirety of God. Through Jesus,
God genuinely experienced human life from within creation---its
limitations, relationships, choices, suffering, and death. We believe
this gave God a deeper experiential understanding of the human condition
and human decision-making.

We believe Jesus was beaten, crucified, truly died, defeated death, was
bodily resurrected, appeared to others as recorded in Scripture, and
ascended. We affirm the ascension without claiming certainty about its
physical mechanics or Jesus's present physical location or state. We
believe the resurrected Jesus will physically return.

Jesus reveals what God is like and what God's Love is willing to do to
understand, find, forgive, and restore what has become separated.

## The Holy Spirit

We believe the Holy Spirit is God's own nonphysical presence throughout
creation, not a separate divine consciousness. Through the Spirit, God's
Love, will, and influence remain present and active even where God's
physical presence is not manifested. God's influence may affect created
beings physically, mentally, and emotionally without overriding genuine
agency.

## Scripture and Interpretation

We believe Scripture was divinely inspired and has been providentially
maintained. Its transmission and translation through human beings
introduce the possibility of fallibility in particular wording and
interpretation, but those limitations have not defeated the overall
purpose for which Scripture was given.

We therefore take Scripture seriously as a source for understanding God
while recognizing the importance of language, literary form, historical
and cultural context, textual transmission, translation, and
interpretation. We distinguish the biblical text from translation,
interpretation, later doctrine, reception, and present-day application.

Jesus and the new covenant's law of Love provide the central Christian
lens through which Scripture and ethics are understood. Where evidence
or interpretation is genuinely disputed, Canonical Shelf should identify
that disagreement rather than manufacture certainty.

## Humanity, Original Sin, and Agency

We believe original sin brought death into the human condition. We
inherit the consequences of that sin, including mortality, but we are
not born personally guilty for the sins of those who came before us.
Human nature will eventually lead each of us into sin, and we become
responsible for our own choices rather than bearing personal guilt for
the choices of our predecessors.

We believe God gives created beings genuine agency. A free choice exists
when a conscious being is capable of deciding how to act. Circumstances
may restrict the available choices or make their consequences
extraordinarily severe, but those circumstances do not themselves make
the decision.

God may influence, guide, prompt, or prepare circumstances without
controlling our response. We may recognize God's influence and still
choose not to follow it. We may freely surrender ourselves to God's
guidance, and that surrender remains voluntary.

## Sin, Intention, and Responsibility

We believe sin is whatever contradicts God's loving will and purpose.
Harm alone does not determine whether an action is sinful. An action may
involve pain, loss, or harm while still serving God's purpose, just as
an action that appears harmless may nevertheless contradict that
purpose.

Intention, knowledge, faith, circumstances, and consequences all matter
when understanding a person's choices, but none alone defines sin. A
loving intention does not automatically make the means righteous, and
harmful consequences do not automatically make the means sinful.

It is not up to us to determine whether a particular action ultimately
serves or contradicts God's larger will and purpose. We are responsible
for the choices we make using the understanding, faith, intention, and
knowledge available to us; judgment belongs to God.

## Faith, Grace, Forgiveness, and Repentance

We believe faith is the conscious decision to believe what cannot
presently be seen, particularly to believe that God exists and is what
God claims to be. Faith provides the foundation for trust: believing
that God is who God claims to be allows us to choose whether to trust
God.

Forgiveness and salvation are gifts of God's Love through Jesus. They
are not rewards earned by religious works, rituals, prescribed words,
moral performance, or human achievement. Faith is not payment for grace,
and works do not purchase it.

Sin remains sin whether or not a person understood it at the time, while
knowledge, intention, understanding, and agency matter to personal
responsibility. When we later understand that a past action contradicted
God's loving purpose, that understanding creates a present
responsibility to respond.

We understand repentance as recognition, ownership without blame or
self-condemnation, apology, and a genuine intention to produce a
different outcome going forward. Successful future behavior is not what
makes repentance genuine.

Guilt is an internal confession that we have done something wrong. Shame
can create a sense of unworthiness that prevents a person from asking
for forgiveness, but Love does not withhold forgiveness because a
wounded person cannot perform the correct words. Asking can be a
meaningful expression of repentance and relationship; it does not cause
God to become willing to forgive.

## Salvation, Resurrection, Judgment, and Reconciliation

We believe Christ has already saved humanity from death. Through Jesus's
death and resurrection, death was defeated and can no longer permanently
hold those who die. Because of Christ's victory over death, all will be
resurrected.

Resurrection is not the same as reconciliation. At judgment, God reveals
the truth of God's existence, God's nature as Love, and what God
accomplished through Jesus. Knowledge alone is not the same as
understanding. Being told that something is wrong does not necessarily
mean understanding why it is contrary to Love. God's revelation makes
genuine understanding and response possible.

Each person remains free to respond. Genuine repentance cannot be
reduced to fear, coercion, or a desire merely to escape suffering. God,
who knows the heart, determines whether repentance is genuine.

We believe God's restorative work ultimately succeeds without destroying
genuine freedom. God's Love remains available even to those who turn
away. We believe all will eventually, through genuine repentance and
their own free choice, seek the Source of Love and be reconciled to God,
until what was lost has been restored and evil itself ceases to exist.

## The New Covenant and the Law of Love

We believe Jesus established a new covenant between God and humanity and
established Love as its governing law. We are called to love God and to
love one another, with Jesus's life and teaching revealing what that
Love looks like in practice.

The laws and covenants recorded earlier in Scripture remain important
for understanding God's relationship with humanity and the biblical
story, but Christian moral responsibility is understood through the new
covenant and its law of Love.

## The Church

We believe the Church is the body of Christ actively transmitting
Christ's message. Its identity is rooted in continuing Christ's work and
message rather than in any particular denomination, institution, or
physical building.

## Baptism and Communion

We believe baptism is a public profession of faith and a personally
significant symbol of new life with Christ. In baptism, a person is
buried in water, symbolically washed of sin, and raised to live a new
life with Christ, reflecting his death, burial, and resurrection.
Baptism is an expression of faith rather than a requirement for
salvation; salvation is not produced by the act of baptism itself.

We believe Christ's table is open to all people. Each person is called
to reflect and freely decide whether to partake. That choice belongs to
the individual: whether someone participates or abstains, the decision
should be respected without argument, and all should be welcomed and
treated equally.

## Inclusion, Relationships, and Ministry

We believe LGBTQ+ people are fully included in the life and body of
Christ. LGBTQ+ identity and faithful same-sex relationships are
compatible with Christian faith and the new covenant's law of Love.
LGBTQ+ people should be welcomed and treated equally without being
required to deny their identity or relationships as a condition of
belonging, participation, leadership, or relationship with God.

We believe women and men may serve equally in every form of Christian
ministry and leadership. Calling, gifting, and capacity to serve are not
determined by gender, and no ministry or leadership role should be
withheld from a person because she is a woman.

## Hope and Open Questions

We believe Christian hope rests in Jesus's resurrection, the
resurrection of the dead, God's righteous judgment, freely chosen
reconciliation, and the ultimate end of evil.

Canonical Shelf does not adopt a definitive interpretation of what the
biblical "image of God" consists of, nor a definitive position
concerning the ultimate physical state, renewal, or transformation of
creation itself.

## How Canonical Shelf Uses This Statement

This Statement of Faith is the **doctrinal ceiling** for Canonical
Shelf. The Course, Topics, Bible study tools, and Theologian may explain
historical Christian doctrines, denominational positions, scholarly
arguments, and interpretations beyond this statement, but they must
distinguish those views from Canonical Shelf's own position and may not
silently establish a contrary doctrine as Canonical Shelf teaching.

The longer internal belief and interpretive document is supplemental
context. It records nuance, reasoning, uncertainty, departures from
traditional formulations, and personal theological conclusions. It does
not replace or outrank this public Statement of Faith.
