## Inclusion: explain disagreement without putting dignity on trial {#orient}
> This guide is affirming. It explains how arguments are made while treating participants’ dignity and welcome as established commitments.

Jesus names love of God and neighbor as central commands and relates them to the Law and Prophets. This provides a governing concern for Christian ethical reading. Love must be explained through conduct and responsibility rather than left as an undefined slogan.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
This guide explicitly affirms LGBTQ dignity, relationships, marriage, and equal participation in ministry. It does not claim every Christian tradition shares that conclusion. Non-affirming readers often appeal to the continuing scope of prohibitions; affirming readers examine ancient contexts, the relationships being described, and the wider ethical witness.

A brief introduction cannot replace close study of every contested passage. It can teach the questions an argument must answer: what conduct is described, who is addressed, what assumptions are present, and how the proposed application relates to love and the dignity of those affected.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
Discussion should represent disagreements accurately while refusing harassment or the demand that LGBTQ participants defend their worth. A community’s interpretation has practical consequences. Evaluate whether its stated welcome includes relationships, leadership, and equal participation rather than relying only on general language.
