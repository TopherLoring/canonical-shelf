## Receiving grace: repentance and trust {#orient}
> Grace is a gift, faith receives it, and changed conduct follows. Receiving help does not mean a person has no value.

Ephesians moves from a description of bondage and death toward God’s mercy and life with Christ. The movement gives divine initiative priority. It does not portray salvation as the final prize awarded to people who have already repaired themselves.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
Grace names the gift-character of salvation. Faith names trust and reception rather than a competing currency with which to purchase it. In this guide, acknowledging sin, repentance, and acceptance of Christ’s saving gift belong to receiving grace, not to establishing a claim that God owes salvation.

The denial of boasting addresses the attempt to make salvation a measure of superiority. Inability to merit salvation must not be turned into the claim that people have no value. The text’s appeal to love and mercy belongs within the explanation, not outside it.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
The movement toward good works remains part of the passage. The order matters: created for a changed life is not the same as saved by accumulating achievements. When explaining the gospel, separate the gift, its reception, and its ethical result while keeping their relationship visible.
