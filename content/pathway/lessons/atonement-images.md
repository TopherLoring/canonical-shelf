## Atonement: several images of one saving work {#orient}
> Reconciliation names restored relationship; penal substitution names bearing a penalty; victory names defeating hostile powers. Each highlights a dimension of Christ’s saving work.

This passage presents reconciliation as God’s initiative through Christ. Reconciliation concerns a relationship restored across estrangement. Its direction matters: the saving work is not described as something humanity first achieves by making itself worthy.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
Paul also speaks of sin and righteousness in relation to Christ and those reconciled through him. Christians draw on this and other passages when explaining representation, substitution, sacrifice, and exchange. A theological model gathers evidence; it is not identical to a single metaphor.

Penal substitution describes Christ as bearing the penalty due to sinners. Victory accounts emphasize the defeat of sin and death; reconciliation emphasizes restored relationship. These themes can overlap. The guide accommodates penal substitution while declining to present it as the only scriptural account of how salvation is accomplished.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
Christ’s necessary sacrifice and resurrection remain central. The lesson concerns what Christ accomplishes; the next lesson considers receiving the gift. Keeping these questions distinct protects the saving work from being reduced either to a transaction diagram or to an inspiring example humanity could reproduce on its own.
