## Resurrection: transformed life rather than escape {#orient}
> Paul speaks of transformed embodied life. His images hold change and continuity together without mapping every future detail.

Paul responds to questions about how the dead are raised and what kind of body is involved. The seed image introduces both continuity and transformation. The image helps readers think, but it is not a detailed biological mechanism for the resurrection.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
The contrasts include perishable and imperishable, weakness and power, natural and spiritual. Spiritual body should not automatically be translated into no body at all. Within the historic teaching used here, resurrection concerns transformed embodied life.

Christian hope does not make present embodied life disposable. The claim that God overcomes death is different from the claim that physical existence never mattered. Care for bodies and grief at death can coexist with hope.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
Resurrection is also distinct from reincarnation into repeated earthly lives. The course teaches the historic resurrection hope and does not incorporate personal recurrence theories as scriptural fact.
