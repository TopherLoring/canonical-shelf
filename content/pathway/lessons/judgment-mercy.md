## Judgment: serious teaching with interpretive limits {#orient}
> Judgment makes care and accountability serious. Explaining its interpretations is different from claiming to know each person’s final destiny.

The scene presents judgment through a king’s separation of groups and their treatment of people in need. Its dramatic form makes conduct toward others impossible to dismiss. Christian teaching about hope includes accountability, not only reassurance.

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
The identity of the least of these and the passage’s relation to wider salvation teaching are debated. A responsible explanation should state the reasons for a reading and compare it with other texts. It should not silently replace the whole Christian account of grace with a score of good deeds.

Christians also differ in how they understand final punishment. Eternal conscious separation or punishment, final destruction, and universal reconciliation are distinct interpretations whose arguments draw on different passages and readings of key language. Describing these does not make this course adopt every view.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
The guide teaches salvation through Christ and the seriousness of judgment without declaring the final destiny of particular individuals. Its practical direction is attentive care, repentance, and hope. Avoid using speculation about the future to evade a need directly in front of you.

**Canonical Shelf’s own position.** The guide’s Statement of Faith holds that Christ has already saved humanity from death, so all will be raised, but that resurrection is not the same as reconciliation. At judgment God reveals who God is and what God accomplished through Jesus, and each person remains free to respond; God, who knows the heart, judges whether repentance is genuine. The Statement expects that all will eventually, freely and genuinely, seek the Source of Love and be reconciled, until evil itself ceases. That is a form of the universal-reconciliation view described above. It is the guide’s position, not a consensus, and the passages the other views rely on are taught here as well.
