## Creeds: learning a shared confession {#orient}
> A creed is a shared summary of belief. Learning what it means is different from being required to profess it.

Paul recalls a message he received and passed on concerning Christ’s death, burial, resurrection, and appearances. The language gives learners an example of a community preserving a central proclamation. It is not a complete later creed, but it helps explain why shared summaries matter.

::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
Creeds state central Christian convictions in a form communities can teach and confess. The historic teaching about Trinity and incarnation in this course belongs to that creedal conversation. A creed gathers an interpretation of Scripture rather than replacing the need to read it.

A confession can also mark boundaries, and Christians differ about the authority given to particular formulations. This course does not require a learner to recite a creed as a personal profession. Accurate understanding and personal assent are distinct outcomes.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
When studying a creed, identify its claims about God, Jesus, salvation, the Church, and hope. Compare each with relevant biblical testimony and ask what misunderstanding a phrase seeks to prevent. Treat unfamiliar wording as an invitation to inquiry rather than a reason to silence questions.
