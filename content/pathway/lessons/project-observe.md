## Project lab 1: observe a whole book {#orient}
> Read the whole book, build a profile, and label what you know versus what you still need to investigate.

Begin with a manageable book and read it completely in Explore. Philemon works well for a first project because its short length permits repeated reading. The selected opening introduces names, relationships, an address, and thanksgiving; the full letter supplies the appeal.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
Build a profile with five fields: summary, people, opening move, authorial claim, and audience. Record what the book itself says separately from traditional attribution or scholarly reconstruction. A familiar title is not always a complete account of authorship or audience.

Locate the book on the shelf, identify its group, and distinguish narrative setting from composition date. These are different forms of information. The integrated Content, Groups, Order, and Chronology missions reinforce these distinctions across all 66 books.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
Your evidence notebook should include references and at least one observation that surprised you. Do not fill a missing field with a guess to make the profile look complete. Mark an uncertainty and identify what source or passage could help investigate it.
