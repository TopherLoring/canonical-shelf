## Become a context detective {#orient}
> Read before and after the verse. Paul is talking about contentment in both need and abundance. Strength here does not mean a promise to succeed at every goal.

A verse can sound very different when detached from its paragraph. Philippians 4:13 is often used as a promise of unlimited success. In verses 10–12, Paul discusses support from others, need, abundance, hunger, and learning contentment. These details define the situation in which he speaks of strength.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
Observation asks what is present in the text. Interpretation asks what it means in context. Application asks how that meaning should shape life now. Moving straight from an isolated phrase to a personal promise skips an important step.

A contextual reading understands Christ’s strength here in relation to enduring changing circumstances. It does not turn the verse into a guarantee that someone will win a competition or avoid illness. This distinction protects people from being blamed when hard circumstances persist.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
Good interpretation can be tested. Name the details that support your reading, notice evidence that complicates it, and ask whether the same reading makes sense across the paragraph. Metaphorical and literal readings alike need reasons. Context is not permission to make a passage say anything.
