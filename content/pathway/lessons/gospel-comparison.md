## Four Gospels: compare without flattening {#orient}
> Read each Gospel as a shaped account. Compare what each writer emphasizes before combining their stories.

Luke’s opening describes earlier accounts, transmitted testimony, investigation, and an ordered presentation. The author states a purpose for writing. This gives beginners a way to ask how a Gospel communicates rather than treating it as a contextless collection of sayings.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
The Gospels share a central subject while arranging material and emphasizing concerns differently. Read a scene within one account before combining it with another. A combined retelling may be useful, but it can hide the reason a particular writer places a scene where they do.

Comparison should record agreement and difference before trying to explain either. Neither immediate harmonization nor an immediate charge of fraud is a substitute for close reading. Distinguish a literary observation from a historical conclusion that would require further evidence.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
Use Explore to compare the openings of Mark and Luke. Record each opening’s starting point, announced concerns, and effect on a new reader. Then identify one question the comparison raises. The goal is a more attentive reading of both accounts, not a contest in which one must be discarded.
