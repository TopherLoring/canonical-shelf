## One body: difference without disposable people {#orient}
> Belonging does not require sameness. The body needs its different members and must attend to those it tends to overlook.

The body image holds unity and difference together. Paul does not solve difference by requiring every member to perform the same function. Neither does he treat distinct functions as a reason for some people to be excluded from belonging.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
The argument challenges two directions of exclusion: a member declaring itself outside because it differs, and another declaring that a member is unnecessary. Both fail to understand the body’s interdependence. The image concerns mutual need as well as identity.

Paul gives particular attention to members treated as weaker or less honorable. The argument therefore does more than celebrate impressive talents. It asks how the community regards those who may be easily overlooked, and how suffering and honor are shared.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
A modern application might examine whose participation is constrained by inaccessible arrangements or whose work goes unnoticed. The passage is not a complete organizational chart. Its argument supplies a reason to question arrangements that treat people as disposable.
