## Project lab 2: trace an argument and a theme {#orient}
> Connect evidence to a claim, test a fair alternative, and trace a theme without erasing the passages’ differences.

Read the appeal after completing the whole-letter profile. Identify what response Paul seeks and how the relationship language supports it. Build an argument map rather than collecting phrases that happen to sound relevant to a preferred conclusion.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
A map should connect observations to a claim and make the warrant for each connection visible. A statement can support a limited interpretation without establishing every larger ethical conclusion. Include at least one piece of evidence that complicates your first reading.

Then trace one of the course’s fourteen themes into another book. Mercy in Philemon can be compared with mercy in a parable, for example, but the two settings must remain distinct. A thread is a developing connection, not permission to make every passage say the same thing.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
Write a short alternative interpretation that an informed reader could recognize as fair. Explain what evidence favors your reading, what remains uncertain, and what would change your mind. The integrated Themes and Content missions supply broader practice; this project requires you to articulate reasons.
