## Authority and unity across Christian traditions {#orient}
> Christians can share central commitments while differing about authority and church structure. Compare the specific claims and their effects.

Ephesians calls for humility, patience, and efforts toward unity. The repeated language of one gives that unity a theological foundation. The passage does not present a modern denominational directory or resolve every later institutional disagreement.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
Christian traditions give different accounts of how Scripture, inherited tradition, communal interpretation, and church leadership relate. Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant approaches also contain internal variety. Describing a source of authority should include how it is interpreted and by whom.

Church governance may involve bishops, councils of elders, or congregational decision-making. Governance and doctrine are related but not identical: knowing who makes a decision does not by itself tell us what decision they will make. Compare specific claims rather than relying only on labels.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
Ecumenical cooperation can involve shared worship, learning, or service while disagreements remain explicit. A fair conversation asks what another tradition affirms and why, then identifies the practical implications. Unity need not require pretending meaningful differences are absent.
