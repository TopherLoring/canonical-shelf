## Put understanding into practice {#orient}
> The Samaritan becomes a neighbor by giving concrete care across a social boundary. The challenge is to recognize and practice mercy, not merely define who deserves it.

A parable is a story that invites its audience to recognize, question, or reconsider something. In this conversation, a legal expert asks about eternal life and then asks who counts as a neighbor. Jesus responds with a traveler who is assaulted and left injured.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
The story names a priest, a Levite, and a Samaritan. The Samaritan’s role unsettles expectations across a real social and religious boundary. The text does not tell us every motive of the people who pass by; explanations that confidently supply those motives go beyond the passage.

Mercy becomes practical: approaching the injured person, tending wounds, transporting him, arranging care, and paying for it. Jesus redirects attention from defining a category of acceptable neighbors to becoming a neighbor through action. Loving intentions alone are not the whole account.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
This guide teaches salvation as God’s gift through Christ’s sacrifice and resurrection, received in repentance and faith rather than earned by good deeds. The parable nevertheless gives discipleship concrete ethical content. Reading grace and responsibility together avoids reducing Christianity either to a merit contest or to words with no consequences.
