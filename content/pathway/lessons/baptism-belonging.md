## Baptism: sign, participation, and community {#orient}
> Baptism connects Christian belonging with Christ’s death and new life. Churches differ about timing and how the practice conveys that meaning.

Paul connects baptism with Christ’s death and resurrection in an argument about a changed life. The passage’s imagery is stronger than describing an arbitrary membership badge. It locates the baptized person’s identity in relation to Christ.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
Christians differ over how baptism relates to grace, faith, and salvation. Some emphasize sacramental participation; others emphasize a confession of faith and symbolic testimony. The guide teaches its spiritual and communal significance without declaring baptism a necessary condition for salvation.

Infant-baptizing communities commonly emphasize divine initiative and nurture within the community. Believer-baptizing communities emphasize a person’s own profession of faith. These are different reasons and practices, not evidence that one side cares about faith while the other does not.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
A beginner can ask what promises are spoken, what the community undertakes, and how the practice is understood locally. Learning those details should make an actual conversation with a church more informed. It should not become a test that excludes someone from this course or its welcome.
