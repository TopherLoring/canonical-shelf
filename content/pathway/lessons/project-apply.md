## Project lab 3: explain, apply, and revisit {#orient}
> Finish with a clear evidence-based brief, a responsible action, and a plan to revisit your understanding.

The closing appeal includes reception, possible debt, confidence, and further personal relationships. Read these details within the whole letter before claiming its implications. A project should finish with more contextual understanding than it had at the beginning.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}
Write a five-part brief: question, context, evidence, interpretation with an alternative, and application with a limit. A short brief can be rigorous if references and reasoning are clear. Length alone is not evidence of understanding.

Choose a concrete response and identify whom it affects. Distinguish a justified application from the passage’s direct wording and check whether it risks recreating harm. An action that sounded loving in abstraction may need revision after listening to the people involved.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}
Return later and explain the book without looking at the first draft, then compare and correct it. Use the verse lab to rebuild a passage and identify its speaker and recipient. Independent study is successful when you can reason, revise, and continue learning; the app does not certify the truth of a private theological conclusion.
