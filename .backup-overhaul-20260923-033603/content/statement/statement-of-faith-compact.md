# Statement of Faith

**Canonical Shelf / TopherLoring Ministries**

> **You are not asked to agree with this Statement of Faith to use Canonical Shelf.** It publishes the convictions that bound Canonical Shelf’s own doctrinal claims. Learning activities assess understanding, evidence, interpretation, and reasoning—not theological assent.

## God

We believe in one God, Creator and source of all that exists, known in Christian faith as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. We receive the doctrine of the Trinity as the historic Christian framework for holding together the biblical witness to one God, the divine identity of Jesus Christ, the work and presence of the Holy Spirit, and the genuine distinction among Father, Son, and Spirit.

## Jesus Christ

We believe Jesus Christ is fully divine and fully human. In him God enters human history, reveals God’s character, announces and embodies God’s reign, and calls people into renewed relationship with God and one another. We believe Jesus was crucified, truly died, was bodily raised from the dead, appeared to his followers, and ascended. His life, teaching, death, and resurrection are the central interpretive lens of Christian faith.

## The Holy Spirit

We believe the Holy Spirit is God’s active and living presence, at work in creation, the Church, and human lives. The Spirit guides, comforts, convicts, gives gifts, and forms people toward love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

## Scripture and interpretation

We believe Scripture is inspired and authoritative for Christian faith. The Bible is a library written across different languages, cultures, genres, communities, and historical settings, so faithful interpretation requires attention to literary form, historical and cultural context, language, translation, and the wider canonical witness.

We distinguish the biblical text from translation, interpretation, later doctrine, reception, and present-day application. We read Scripture through the character and teaching of Jesus Christ, especially his commands to love God and neighbor and his concern for mercy, justice, faithfulness, human dignity, and the fruit produced by religious teaching. Where evidence or interpretation is genuinely disputed, Canonical Shelf should say so rather than manufacture certainty.

## Humanity, sin, grace, and salvation

We believe every human being possesses inherent dignity and is worthy of love and respect. Sin is real and includes the ways human beings turn from God and harm themselves, other people, communities, and creation through selfishness, exploitation, violence, injustice, dishonesty, domination, hatred, cruelty, abuse of power, faithlessness, and failure to love.

We believe salvation is God’s gift through Jesus Christ. It cannot be earned through moral performance, social conformity, or perfect theology. Grace comes first; repentance, healing, reconciliation, justice, growth, and discipleship follow as responses to grace rather than the price of receiving it.

## Covenant, ethics, and discipleship

We believe Christians live within the new covenant established through Christ and should not treat every command given within ancient Israel’s covenant as a timeless Christian command without theological and historical reasoning. Freedom from the Mosaic covenant is not freedom from moral responsibility. Christian ethics should be tested through Scripture, the character and teaching of Jesus, love of neighbor, justice, mercy, faithfulness, human dignity, the work of the Spirit, and the fruit an interpretation or practice produces.

Discipleship is lifelong formation in the way of Christ. No one must become spiritually finished before they may learn, worship, participate, seek God, or belong.

## The Church and the open table

We believe the Church is called to worship, teaching, prayer, baptism, Communion, service, generosity, reconciliation, justice, and shared life in Christ. Christian communities differ over sacramental theology, governance, ministry, interpretation, and many other questions; those disagreements should be represented accurately rather than reduced to stereotypes.

We believe belonging can precede certainty. People may approach Christian faith with confidence, doubt, disagreement, questions, wounds, another religious background, or none at all. Conviction should not require coercion, and disagreement should not require dehumanization.

## Inclusion, relationships, and ministry

We affirm LGBTQ+ people as fully welcome within Christian community. We do not believe homosexual or bisexual orientation is itself sinful or something a person must renounce in order to receive grace, follow Christ, worship, serve, lead, form a family, or participate fully in Christian community.

We believe faithful same-sex relationships and marriages can embody Christian virtues such as love, fidelity, mutuality, honesty, responsibility, consent, equality, self-control, care, dignity, and self-giving commitment. We recognize that Christians interpret the relevant biblical texts differently and require Canonical Shelf to represent serious competing readings accurately while stating its own affirming position clearly.

We affirm the full dignity, giftedness, participation, leadership, and spiritual agency of women. Gender is not a basis for denying a person’s capacity to teach, lead, serve, or respond to God’s calling.

## Hope

We believe Christian hope rests in the resurrection of Jesus Christ, the resurrection of the dead, God’s righteous judgment, reconciliation and restoration, and the ultimate renewal of creation. We entrust final judgment to God, whose justice and mercy are more complete than our own, and we resist using judgment as a tool for fear, exclusion, or declaring convenient groups uniquely condemned.

## How Canonical Shelf uses this statement

This Statement of Faith is the **doctrinal ceiling** for Canonical Shelf. The Course, Topics, Bible study tools, and Theologian may explain historical Christian doctrines, denominational positions, scholarly arguments, and interpretations beyond this statement, but they must distinguish those views from Canonical Shelf’s own position and may not silently establish a contrary doctrine as Canonical Shelf teaching.

The longer belief and interpretive document retained by Canonical Shelf is supplemental context for the Theologian. It may provide nuance, reasoning, pastoral framing, and additional positions, but it does not replace or outrank this compact Statement of Faith.
