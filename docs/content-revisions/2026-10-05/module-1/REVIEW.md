# Reading the Bible Well — editorial review

All 13 existing lessons have revised script drafts. Their stable IDs, objectives, section titles and anchors, readings, checks and answer logic, reflections, drawers, and deeper notes are retained. Glossary entries retain their original terms and now use complete explanatory sentences. These files are review copies outside the build.

The revisions retain the content while changing its delivery: an idea is introduced, placed in its setting, and explained before the script moves to the next section. Existing summary slogans have been incorporated into the opening prose rather than repeated alongside it. The teaching-prose length check is separate from front matter and activity data; longer text is not itself evidence of better teaching.

A further opening pass removed repeated restatements of the central premise, title, or objective. Each opening now supplies its subject and setting without rehearsing the same setup through several paraphrases; distinct details are retained in the opening or the substantive sections.

## Factual wording and source notes

- The script now describes Paul's summary as **one of the earliest surviving written statements**, rather than claiming it is definitively the earliest or shortest. The transmission language in 1 Corinthians 15 supports received and passed on; it does not independently establish a complete chronology of every early formulation.
- The original drawer “Why start with Paul and not with Genesis?” still contains the stronger earliest claim. Drawer text was preserved under the content-preservation instruction; this remaining wording should be corrected in a separately reviewed apparatus edit before publication.
- Gospel is described directly as translating Greek *euangelion*. The Old English history has been omitted in accordance with Chris's clarification. The lexical sense is supported by [the LSJ entry hosted by Perseus](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn%3Acite2%3Ascaife-viewer%3Adictionaries.v1%3Alsj-n43315/).
- The sample's historical context is reflected through public good-news announcements. [The Priene calendar inscription presented by Bibelhaus Frankfurt](https://www.bibelhaus-frankfurt.de/en/bimumag/the-special-object/calendar-inscription-from-priene) supplies an imperial example connected with Augustus's birth and public honors; it should not be misdescribed as specifically an accession proclamation. The comparison explains the weight of the announcement; it does not claim Paul copied that inscription or that the term had only political uses.
- Patronage remains background for questions about status, not proof of individual motives. The existing Jennings reference in the deeper note has been preserved but not independently audited bibliographically in this wording pass.
- Genre and date debates remain explicitly contested. The revision does not resolve the historicity of Genesis, Jonah, or Job, or the dating of Daniel.

## Sequence and coverage observations

The first two Bible-library lessons overlap with library, genre, and shelf-skills. The drafts preserve all five lessons while distinguishing their jobs: overview of groups, overview of literary forms, navigation and editions, practice in a particular poem, and timeline/skill orientation. No lesson has been merged or retired.

The outline includes an empty c1.synthesis unit. The approved curriculum design also names proposed detailed early question lessons that are not individually present in this 13-lesson source inventory. These are structural coverage questions, not material silently added to this revision.

The apparatus refers to an extra-credit module, which is approved in the curriculum design but has no authored source lessons in the current four-module inventory. Existing references have been retained; they should not be read as evidence that the extra-credit content already exists.

## Review limits

Automated preservation verifies structural identity and retained activity data, not the quality of exegesis or complete teaching-point equivalence. Human editorial and theological review is still needed before these drafts enter the learner-facing build. Card division has deliberately not been applied.
