## Three ways to place a book {#orient}
A book's shelf position, narrative setting, and composition answer different questions. Shelf order tells you where to find it in this edition. Narrative setting concerns when its events occur, while composition concerns when the writing took shape. Knowing one does not automatically establish the others.

The 66-book collection used here has nine navigation groups: Law, History, Wisdom and Poetry, Major Prophets, Minor Prophets, Gospels and Acts, Paul's Letters, General Letters, and Revelation. These are organizational labels rather than nine pure genres; one book can contain narrative, law, poetry, or vision. The seven original skill tracks connect this orientation with the contextual reading you will need as the biblical story begins.

## Shelf order, story order, and writing order {#timeline}
The first three original skill tracks develop orientation. Story traces ten broad movements alongside the relevant books. Order practices the groups, the two testaments, and the full collection of 66 books. Groups connects a shelf's boundaries with the approaches useful for reading it. Luke's references to Law, Prophets, and Psalms place this app's arrangement alongside another way of describing Israel's Scriptures.

Chronology then distinguishes shelf position, the time narrated, and the time of composition. Content adds summaries, characters, openings, authorship, and audiences. Dates and attributions differ in certainty, so a timeline should identify what is debated rather than presenting every placement as equally settled. The integrated skill missions retain this practice while marking those limits.

Job illustrates the distinction. The book appears among Wisdom and Poetry, while its story resembles the world of the early ancestors in Genesis. The book's date and development remain debated and may be much later than the setting portrayed. Knowing all three placements gives a reader better questions to ask than knowing the page number alone.

## The skills you will use from here {#skills}
Themes follows fourteen threads across the books, including covenant, wilderness, remnant, presence, worship, and mission. Verses connects passages with their themes, speakers, recipients, and reconstructed wording. These tracks complete the seven skills by helping recall serve an understanding of the passage.

Remembering a verse accurately does not establish that an interpretation is sound. The recalled wording still belongs to a speaker, an audience, and a setting. Likewise, a theme helps connect passages only when those connections respect what each passage is doing.

Review & Practice reinforces what the Learning Path teaches. Use the skills to make reading more informed: find the book, recognize its setting, and relate its wording to its context. Their value lies in that reading work, rather than in turning recall alone into a judgment about understanding.

## What you now know {#summary}
You can place a book by shelf order, narrative setting, and composition, and explain why those placements answer different questions. You also know how the seven skills support reading: Story, Order, and Groups develop orientation; Chronology and Content add setting and information; Themes and Verses strengthen connections and recall.

The broad sequence of the biblical story is easier to establish than the dates of many books. Where a date is debated, the guide identifies that uncertainty rather than removing it from the timeline. The next unit begins the story from creation onward. As you open each book, ask which placement you need and what evidence supports it.
