# Module 4 editorial review

These 32 scripts are review copies of the existing lessons. They do not enter the application build. Each source was read before revision. The revisions retain the existing teaching scope, doctrinal positions, examples, uncertainty, and opposing readings. No new lesson, historical digression, or denominational identity has been introduced.

The opening epigraphs have been incorporated into connected prose instead of repeating a title, caption, and introduction. Each opening establishes a subject and its textual setting; the following sections explain the claims and their consequences. Existing glossary terms now have complete explanatory sentences. The glossary changes remain within material already present in the source lessons.

## Actual sequence and coverage issues

The module mixes retained introductory maps with later synthesis. `c1-theology-map` precedes the specialist lessons, but `doctrine` recaps Trinity, incarnation, and providence after those ideas have already been introduced. `incarnation-humanity` sits after this recap in the providence unit rather than alongside `trinity-language`. These are genuine overlaps and placement questions; the scripts have not been moved or retired.

`atonement-images`, `receiving-grace`, and `grace-repair` develop different aspects of salvation, and `cross-grace` then recapitulates all three. The recap can consolidate the distinction between accomplishment, reception, and response, but should be recognized as synthesis rather than another first introduction.

The traditions sequence includes `c1-traditions-map`, `traditions-welcome`, `traditions`, `creeds-reading`, and `authority-unity`. Fair comparison, internal diversity, and cooperation recur substantially. Covenant frameworks interrupt that sequence with a different Israel/Church question. Its anti-boasting theme then recurs more directly in `jewish-roots`.

`hard-questions` surveys much of the difficult-text unit after `ethical-texts`, but before the more focused inclusion, Jewish-roots, and suffering lessons. The overview/focused treatment relationship needs a deliberate sequence decision. No such decision is implied by this wording revision.

The practice map introduces Communion, prayer, worship, formation, and generosity, but this module's named authored practice lessons are baptism and neighbor-care. The inventory does not contain dedicated lessons here for every practice mentioned. The existing map's promises should be checked against the actual curriculum rather than treated as fulfilled by this revision.

The project sequence has an overview, lab 1, and lab 3. No lab 2 appears in this module's inventory. The existing IDs and titles remain intact; no missing lesson was fabricated.

Final-hope coverage repeats resurrection, renewed-creation imagery, and present care across `apocalypse-hope`, `final-hope`, and `resurrection-body`. The frameworks lesson contributes a distinct taxonomy, while `judgment-mercy` contributes the judgment scene and competing final-punishment accounts.

## Factual and evidentiary limits

The source title and opening heading for `traditions-welcome` say **Seven traditions**, while its objective, body, and a scenario title specify **five** and name five. The revised body explicitly describes those five. The source title, heading, and protected assessment remain unchanged so that this inconsistency is visible for owner review rather than silently changing protected metadata.

Several sources still contain six-course or numbered-unit references. Obsolete course promises in editable body prose have been restated as the same pedagogical progression without those numbers. Protected drawers and deeper notes retain their original references, including Course 6 and Units 3/9. Those links or labels require a separate metadata review against the current four-module structure.

`final-hope` assigns 1 Corinthians 15:20–28 but discusses spiritual-body contrasts occurring later in the chapter. The revision now identifies those as elsewhere in Paul's resurrection argument; `resurrection-body` supplies the relevant 15:35–49 reading. This clarifies source scope without changing a reading or adding an argument.

`inclusion-reading` remains an introductory methodological lesson. It does not provide a full passage-by-passage affirming case, three supporting texts, historic precedent, or a developed comparison of opposing passages. Its stated commitment and description of interpretive approaches have been retained; a wording-only revision cannot supply missing doctrinal evidence. The same distinction between overview and substantiated case matters when reading broad theological claims elsewhere in this module.

No novel historical dates, lexical derivations, or quotations have been added. No independent web verification is claimed. Existing historical summaries about denominational emphases, covenant systems, creedal development, and nineteenth-century dispensationalism remain inherited source claims, not newly researched findings. The Zacchaeus tense dispute, identity of the least of these, final-punishment arguments, and the guide's distinct theological positions remain explicitly qualified; this revision does not resolve their underlying evidence questions.
