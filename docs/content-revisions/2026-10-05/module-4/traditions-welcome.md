## Seven traditions: compare claims and local practice {#orient}

This lesson considers five traditions: ELCA Lutheran, United Church of Christ, United Methodist, Episcopal, and Alliance of Baptists. The aim is to compare official belief, local practice, and the questions that matter in an actual congregation. These traditions are not interchangeable. A national statement cannot automatically describe every local practice, and a denominational label begins an inquiry rather than completing it. Read Romans 14 with that distinction in mind, while first identifying the differing practices addressed in Paul's own setting.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}

ELCA teaching gives particular attention to justification and law and gospel. Methodist teaching emphasizes grace and sanctification, or growth in holiness and transformed life. Episcopal identity includes Anglican worship and a tradition of common prayer. These are starting emphases rather than exclusive possessions; each community also speaks about Scripture, discipleship, and shared life. An emphasis can help orient comparison without defining everything a tradition believes.

United Church of Christ and Alliance communities give significant room to local congregational life while relating through wider structures. Shared terms such as welcome and inclusion therefore require specific questions. What does an official statement commit a congregation to, and how much room do local congregations have to differ? Examine the statements rather than turning an emphasis into a stereotype. The distinction between wider identity and local practice explains why a warm general description may leave important questions unanswered.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}

Canonical Shelf is explicitly LGBTQ-affirming and open-table, grounded in its own Statement of Faith rather than any one of these traditions. Romans 14 teaches receiving others without treating every difference as grounds for contempt. It does not settle every modern controversy, erase harmful conduct, or remove doctrinal distinctions. Ask a local congregation about welcome, leadership, sacraments, and the handling of questions. Specific answers about participation give practical meaning to a general claim. The objective is an informed comparison, not an assumption that every tradition or every local community practices welcome identically.
