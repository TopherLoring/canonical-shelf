## Inclusion: explain disagreement without putting dignity on trial {#orient}

Jesus names love of God and neighbor as central commands and relates them to the Law and Prophets. This gives Christian ethical reading a governing concern, but love must be explained through conduct and responsibility rather than left as a slogan. Canonical Shelf is affirming: participants' dignity and welcome are established commitments. Our objective is to explain how the guide reasons ethically while representing disagreement accurately, without asking a participant to defend their human worth.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}

The guide explicitly affirms LGBTQ dignity, relationships, marriage, and equal participation in ministry. It does not claim that every Christian tradition shares its conclusion. Non-affirming readers often appeal to the continuing scope of prohibitions. Affirming readers examine ancient contexts, the relationships being described, and the wider ethical witness. Scope means the range of people or conduct to which a claim applies; disagreement about it requires an argument rather than an assumption.

This introduction cannot replace close study of each contested passage. It can identify the questions a reading must answer: what conduct is described, who is addressed, what assumptions are present, and how a proposed application relates to love and the dignity of those affected. Love does not cancel the work of interpreting specific texts. A careful explanation makes its textual reasoning and practical effects visible.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}

Discussion can represent disagreement without permitting harassment or requiring LGBTQ participants to justify their worth. Analytical rigor concerns claims and evidence. Interpretation also has consequences for communal life, so stated welcome must be examined in practice. Does it include relationships, leadership, and equal participation, or does general language leave those questions unanswered? The connection between interpretation and conduct returns us to the objective: transparent reasoning should explain a community's commitments and their effects while preserving participants' dignity.
