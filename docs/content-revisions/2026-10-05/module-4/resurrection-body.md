## Resurrection: transformed life rather than escape {#orient}

Paul answers questions about how the dead are raised and what kind of body is involved. His seed comparison holds continuity and transformation together: there is a connection between what exists now and what is changed. The image helps explain the claim, but it does not supply a detailed biological mechanism for resurrection. Our objective is to understand transformed embodied life without reducing it to ghostly existence or pressing every feature of an analogy into a future specification.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}

Paul contrasts perishable and imperishable, weakness and power, natural and spiritual. Imperishable means not subject to the decay described in the contrast. The phrase spiritual body should not automatically be made to mean no body at all. Within the historic teaching used here, the concern is transformed embodied life, so an explanation must retain embodiment as well as change.

This hope does not make present bodies disposable. Claiming that God overcomes death differs from claiming that physical existence never mattered. Care for bodies and grief at death can therefore coexist with hope. A person need not dismiss suffering now in order to understand the transformation Paul describes.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}

Resurrection also differs from reincarnation into repeated earthly lives. The course teaches historic resurrection hope rather than incorporating personal recurrence theories as scriptural fact. State that distinction while acknowledging what Paul's images do not specify about future life.
