## Resurrection, judgment, and renewed creation {#orient}

Christian hope centers on resurrection and life with God. Paul connects Christ's resurrection with the future of those who belong to him, describing death as an enemy to be overcome. This concerns God's saving action, not a human technique for escaping mortality or a change of address that makes embodied life irrelevant. Our objective is to distinguish resurrection and renewed-creation imagery from disputed schedules and repeated earthly lives, while retaining the questions Christians continue to debate.

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}

Elsewhere in Paul's resurrection argument, contrasts describe transformed embodied life. A spiritual body should not automatically mean an immaterial ghost: the language compares conditions of embodied existence. Christian traditions debate details, but bodily resurrection is the historic emphasis taught here. The transformation does not require treating present bodily life as something that never mattered.

Revelation's visions culminate in renewed creation, divine presence, and the ending of death and tears. The returning tree of life connects final hope with Genesis imagery. These symbolic and apocalyptic forms require contextual interpretation rather than confident assignments of beasts, numbers, or catastrophe to each day's headlines. A narrative connection illuminates meaning without specifying every physical mechanism of the future.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}

Judgment, salvation through Christ, and eternal life are substantive Christian teachings. Christians disagree about the nature and duration of hell, the unevangelized, and detailed sequences of final events. The guide explains principal arguments without claiming knowledge of particular individuals' destiny.

Resurrection differs from reincarnation or repeated earthly lives, which this curriculum does not teach. Canonical Shelf's Statement of Faith holds that God's Love remains available after death: all will be raised, and the guide expects all eventually to be reconciled to God through genuine repentance and their own free choice. That is its position rather than Christian consensus. Many Christians instead understand a person's choice as final at death, and that account is also presented accurately. The guide takes no definitive position on creation's final physical state. Its practical emphasis is hope encouraging faithfulness, care, and justice now, rather than speculation that distracts from responsibility.
