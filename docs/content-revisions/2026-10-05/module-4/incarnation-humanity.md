## Incarnation: fully divine and fully human {#orient}

Incarnation means the Son's becoming human in Jesus Christ. John's opening places the Word in relation to God and then speaks of the Word becoming flesh. The movement holds divine identity together with embodied life. Our objective is to explain why the historic confession preserves both full divinity and full humanity. Calling flesh an irrelevant outer costume would remove the embodied reality that John's language brings into the account.

## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}

Historic Christianity confesses Jesus as fully divine and fully human. It does not describe two incomplete halves combining into an unusual third kind of being. Each half would lack something the confession expressly affirms. Nor is human life merely a visible performance without genuine human experience; such an explanation preserves appearance while losing the reality of humanity.

John also links Jesus with grace, truth, and making God known. Revelation means making known what would otherwise remain hidden. Incarnation therefore concerns revelation as well as presence: Christians look to Jesus to understand God's character. The course reads human life, suffering, and self-giving in light of this confession, because genuine humanity cannot be discarded whenever its consequences become difficult to explain.

## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}

Compare John's presentation with Philippians 2:5–11. Ask what each emphasizes before drawing the accounts into a summary. John's explicit language and the later vocabulary of incarnation are related, but they are different literary acts. A disciplined explanation keeps humanity and divinity in view instead of using one to cancel the other. It can acknowledge philosophical questions it has not settled while still identifying why a disguise or a mixture fails to preserve the claim. Return to that distinction as you explain the lesson: does the account maintain genuine human life as well as divine identity?
