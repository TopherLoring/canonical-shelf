## Day of Atonement: cleansing sacred space and community {#orient}
Leviticus 16 describes the annual Day of Atonement as a coordinated set of rites centered on the high priest and sanctuary. This lesson follows how the chapter connects human wrongdoing and impurity with the cleansing of sacred space and the community’s relationship with God. The day cannot be reduced to a single action without losing the distinctions among cleansing, confession, removal, and restored order. Reading those actions together explains why the sanctuary and community appear within the same ritual sequence.



## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

Read the chapter as a sequence and ask who acts, where the action takes place, and which animal is involved. In particular, keep the two goats separate. The chapter gives them distinct roles, and those roles explain different parts of the day.



## Two goats, two roles {#two-goats}
One goat is used in sanctuary purification and atonement rites; the other bears Israel’s confessed wrongs away into the wilderness. The traditional term scapegoat names the goat sent away. The two actions are coordinated, but they are not identical sacrifices. Preserving the distinction allows us to see both the cleansing of sacred space and the symbolic removal of wrongdoing rather than explaining every feature through a single ritual action.

## Entering the innermost room {#the-innermost-room}
The high priest has distinctive responsibilities, including this exceptional entry into the innermost sanctuary. The entry is not routine access to a room. Its place within the rite connects restricted access and danger with cleansing, confession, removal, and restored order. The conditions of entry therefore matter to understanding the day’s relationship between priest, sanctuary, and community.

## How Hebrews reads this day {#hebrews}
Later Christian interpretation, especially Hebrews, reuses the Day of Atonement’s imagery. That reuse is easier to follow once Leviticus has been read on its own terms. Learn the earlier relationships among priest, sanctuary, cleansing, and access before asking how Hebrews makes them part of its later argument.



## What you now know {#summary}
The annual rites address the sanctuary and community together. The high priest’s exceptional entry, the goat used in sanctuary rites, and the goat carrying confessed wrongs into the wilderness each contribute a distinct part of the sequence. The day remains the holiest in Jewish life, known as Yom Kippur. Christians interpret its imagery through Hebrews in several ways, while the precise ritual mechanics and terminology remain debated.
