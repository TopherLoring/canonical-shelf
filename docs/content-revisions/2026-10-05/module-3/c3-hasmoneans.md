## Hasmonean rule: resistance becomes a dynasty {#orient}

The Hasmoneans are the ruling house that develops from the Maccabean movement. Their history asks what changes when resistance becomes government. The revolt against Seleucid control eventually brings Jewish self-rule, but leaders combine military, political, and priestly authority. That combination creates new controversies alongside the achievement of self-government.



## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}

As Hasmonean territory expands, rulers must deal with diplomacy, warfare, competing groups, and the legitimacy of their priestly authority. Legitimacy means a claim to rightful rule. A movement resisting foreign domination now has to explain why its own dynasty should hold power and how its political authority relates to Temple leadership.

This is why later arguments about priesthood, kingship, law, purity, and authority have historical weight. Different Jewish groups remember and evaluate recent rulers differently. Their disagreements concern institutions and decisions they have lived with, rather than only abstract religious principles. Jewish self-rule is significant, but it does not produce agreement about how that rule should operate.



## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}

Internal Hasmonean conflict eventually opens a way for Roman intervention. Pompey enters Jerusalem in 63 BCE, and Judea moves into a new imperial relationship. This transition frames the later world of Herod and the Gospels. Follow the broad movement from crisis to revolt, dynasty, and Roman involvement while remembering that titles, territory, and degrees of independence change within the Hasmonean period.
