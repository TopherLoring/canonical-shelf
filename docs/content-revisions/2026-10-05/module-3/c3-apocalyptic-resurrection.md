## Apocalyptic hope and resurrection before Christianity {#orient}

Resurrection means the raising of the dead to transformed life. Daniel 12 contains one of the Hebrew Bible’s clearest expressions of this hope: those sleeping in the dust awaken to different destinies. The passage belongs to visions concerned with severe conflict and divine justice. It gives us a Jewish scriptural setting for a question that becomes central to Christian claims about Jesus.



## Read the passage with the question in view {#read}
::reading

## Locate the claim in context {#locate-the-claim-in-context}

Second Temple Jewish writings develop varied expectations concerning resurrection, judgment, angels, cosmic conflict, kingdoms, and the age to come. Shared attention to these questions does not produce one agreed account. Acts’ disagreement between Pharisees and Sadducees over resurrection is one reminder of the differences among Jewish groups.

Apocalyptic literature uses visionary forms to unveil earthly crisis in relation to heavenly realities and divine rule. Symbols, beasts, numbers, heavenly scenes, and dramatic transformations enlarge the frame within which oppression is understood. This explains why genre matters: the imagery makes a theological argument about conflict and hope rather than merely offering a coded timetable for current headlines.



## Keep the setting visible {#keep-the-setting-visible}

Early Christian resurrection claims enter this existing Jewish conversation while making a distinctive claim about Jesus. Their background does not erase that distinctiveness; it helps us understand what is being claimed. Later Gospel study brings Jesus’ resurrection into this setting, and the study of final hope considers its wider Christian significance.
