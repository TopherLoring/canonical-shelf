# Follow the larger story

Lesson ID: story

**Objective:** Connect Jesus’ proclamation to Israel’s story without erasing Judaism.

**Primary reading:** Mark 1:14–15

## Follow the larger story
The larger story Christians trace through the Bible moves from creation and human estrangement through God's covenant with Israel, Israel's life and crises, Jesus' ministry, and the early Church to hope for renewed creation. This lesson provides the whole map before the following lessons examine its opening chapters more closely.

The map follows relationships and turning points, rather than the dates when books were written. The collection is arranged partly by literary group, so its shelf order is not a simple timeline. Placing a passage within the story helps you read it while still asking separately when and how its book took shape.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Mark 1:14–15 (BSB).

Mark's account of Jesus' first preaching belongs within the larger story. Notice how the announcement connects a time that has arrived, a kingdom that has come near, and an invitation to respond. Those elements will make more sense when read alongside Israel's covenants and hopes.

## Covenant, exile, and the prophets
Covenant describes a binding relationship with commitments. God's dealings with Abraham, Israel at Sinai, and David give later biblical hopes their vocabulary. Reading a later promise without knowing those relationships can make it sound detached from the story that explains it.

Exile describes displacement from a homeland. The prophets address injustice, judgment, and hope within the concrete circumstances of Israel's history and its crises. Their words belong to those circumstances before later readers apply them elsewhere. Covenant and exile therefore locate the hopes that form the background to Jesus' announcement.

## Jesus announces God’s kingdom
Jesus announces God's kingdom. Here kingdom concerns God's reign and its realization, rather than simply a location people reach after death. The announcement connects what God is doing with a response from those who hear it.

Mark names repentance and faith. Repentance means turning or reorienting life toward God, so it involves more than shame. Within the larger map, Jesus' preaching brings Israel's hopes into the foreground and asks his hearers to respond to the reign he announces. The narrative landmarks that follow should be ordered as parts of that story, not as dates of composition.

## Jesus within Judaism
Jesus and his earliest followers were Jewish. Their language about covenant, kingdom, and fulfillment belongs within Jewish Scripture and history. A Christian map of the Bible should help explain that connection, rather than removing Jesus from it.

Christian interpretations must not assign collective guilt for Jesus' death to Jewish people or assume that God abandoned them. Judaism remains a living tradition with its own readings of these Scriptures. Locating Christian claims within that history preserves the setting needed to understand them and avoids treating Israel as a story that simply disappears when Jesus arrives.

## What you now know
The larger Christian narrative moves from creation and estrangement through God's covenant with Israel, Israel's life and crises, Jesus and the kingdom, and the early Church to hope for what comes. Covenant names committed relationship; exile locates displacement and crisis; God's kingdom concerns his reign; repentance names a reorientation of life.

Christians broadly share this outline but differ about how fulfillment in Jesus relates to Israel's continuing covenant and what final hope involves. Canonical Shelf leaves creation's final physical state open. Carry the map forward as a way to ask where a passage belongs, while keeping its particular setting and the living Jewish tradition in view.

## Glossary

**Covenant.** A covenant is a committed relationship involving promises and responsibilities. The biblical covenants with Abraham, Israel at Sinai, and David help explain the language of later hopes.

**Kingdom of God.** The kingdom of God concerns God's reign and its realization. In Jesus' announcement it calls for a response in the present, rather than referring only to a place people go after death.

**Repentance.** Repentance means turning away from sin and toward God. It involves a reorientation of life, rather than only feeling ashamed about an action.

## Deeper reading note

A Christian narrative overview is an interpretation of the collection. Compare it with how an individual book presents its own concerns. Israel is more than background scenery for the New Testament.
