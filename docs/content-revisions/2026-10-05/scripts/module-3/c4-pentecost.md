**Objective:** Interpret Pentecost within the Jewish festival of Weeks and trace how Spirit, languages, diaspora, and mission converge in Acts 2.

**Primary reading:** Acts 2:1–13

## Pentecost: Spirit, festival, languages, and mission

Pentecost is a Greek-derived name associated with the Jewish festival of Weeks. Acts 2 begins within that existing pilgrimage festival rather than at the creation of a new Christian date. The earlier study of Israel’s sacred calendar helps explain why people from many regions are in Jerusalem. This setting connects the coming of the Spirit with a gathered audience and a widening mission.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Acts 2:1–13 (BSB).

## Locate the claim in context

Acts describes the Spirit, wind and fire imagery, speech in other languages, and listeners from different linguistic backgrounds. Read these details together. Their narrative connection emphasizes communication across difference rather than a private spiritual spectacle separated from witness.

The list of peoples and regions makes diaspora geography visible. Jewish communities outside their ancestral homeland are represented in the audience at Jerusalem. This breadth anticipates the movement of Acts, where Jerusalem becomes the starting point for witness across linguistic, ethnic, and geographic boundaries. Festival, language, and geography are consequently parts of one scene rather than incidental details.

## Keep the setting visible

Christians interpret Pentecost differently in relation to later experiences of the Spirit, charismatic gifts, baptism in the Spirit, and church order. First establish what this passage narrates before comparing those systems. The Jewish festival setting and the anticipation of widening witness are clear features of the account. Applying every physical sign as an identical requirement for later Christians would require further argument.

## Glossary

**Pentecost.** Pentecost is a Greek-derived name associated with the Jewish festival of Weeks. Acts 2 places the Spirit’s coming within this festival setting.

**Diaspora.** Diaspora refers to communities outside their ancestral homeland. Acts’ audience list makes this wide Jewish geography visible.

**Holy Spirit.** In Christian Scripture and doctrine, the Holy Spirit is the Spirit of God. Later theological study develops the formulations in greater depth.

## Additional questions

### Why Course 2 matters here

Without Israel’s festival calendar, “Pentecost” can sound like a purely Christian event name. The earlier course supplies the Jewish sacred-time context that Acts assumes.

## Deeper reading note

Acts 2 has generated major theological debates about gifts and ecclesiology. Those debates should not be imported into every narrative detail before the passage’s literary movement is understood.
