# Parables: hearing the question behind the story

Lesson ID: parables-welcome

**Objective:** Interpret parables through their surrounding conversation and central comparison.

**Primary reading:** Luke 15:1–10

## Parables: hearing the question behind the story

A parable is a story or comparison that invites insight through a familiar situation. In Luke 15, the situation around Jesus supplies the question the stories address. People approach him, and others complain about his welcome and shared meals. Reading a sheep or coin by itself can miss that conversation. Begin with the complaint, then follow the stories’ answer.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Luke 15:1–10 (BSB).

## Locate the claim in context

Both stories move from a search to finding and celebration. The repeated movement makes recovery a reason for joy. That pattern helps us ask how the stories challenge resentment of Jesus’ welcome. It also gives a stronger basis for interpretation than assigning a separate hidden doctrine to each incidental object.

The stories engage imagination as well as direct instruction. A reader may identify with someone responsibly keeping what matters, then have to reconsider the response to recovering someone previously dismissed. Identification can become unsettling because the joy at the story’s end calls for a response. The parable is doing more than describing a search; it brings that search and its celebration into the complaint about welcome.

## Keep the setting visible

The course’s practice of welcome can draw on this challenge, while distinguishing Luke’s setting from every modern admission debate. Ask whether a community celebrates restoration or resents the attention given to people beyond its favored circle. This question connects present practice with the stories’ concern. A responsible application also states its limits instead of assuming that every contemporary disagreement is already the same situation.

## Glossary

**Parable.** A parable is a story or comparison that invites insight through a familiar situation. Its surrounding conversation helps identify its concern.

**Literary setting.** Literary setting is the surrounding material that shapes how a passage is understood. In Luke 15, the complaint about welcome matters.

**Central comparison.** The central comparison is the main relationship through which a story illuminates its concern. It guides interpretation of the details.

## Deeper reading note

Not every detail must stand for a separate doctrine. Test an interpretation by whether it explains the complaint, the search, and the celebration together.
