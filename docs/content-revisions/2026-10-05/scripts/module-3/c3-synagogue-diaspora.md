## Synagogue and diaspora: Jewish life beyond the Temple

The Jerusalem Temple has a unique role, but Jewish community life also takes place far beyond it. Synagogues provide settings for assembly, Scripture reading, teaching, prayer, and communal activity in Judea, Galilee, and the diaspora. Diaspora refers to communities living outside an ancestral homeland. These institutions and communities help explain how Jewish life remains connected across a wide geography.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Luke 4:16–21 (BSB).

## Locate the claim in context

Synagogue institutions develop over time, and evidence differs from place to place. We should not assume that every gathering has the same organization. The Gospels and Acts nevertheless present synagogue settings as familiar places for reading, teaching, debate, and community response. Their narratives show why these settings matter without supplying a complete institutional history.

Jewish diaspora communities live throughout the Mediterranean and Near East. Many use Greek extensively while maintaining Scriptures, practices, local communal institutions, and connections with Jerusalem. Language and religious identity are different dimensions of life. Greek usage therefore does not make a community less Jewish or remove its relationship with the Temple.

## Keep the setting visible

This wider geography becomes important when we read Acts and Paul. Early Christian mission often enters cities where Jewish communities already have networks, Scriptures, debates about Gentile participation, and places of encounter. These existing relationships help explain the movement’s routes and discussions. Keep Jerusalem’s unique sanctuary and the many local settings in the same picture; they illuminate different aspects of Jewish life rather than canceling one another.

## Glossary

**Synagogue.** A synagogue is a Jewish communal institution for assembly, Scripture, teaching, prayer, and local life. Its institutional form develops across different places and times.

**Diaspora Judaism.** Diaspora Judaism refers to Jewish communities living outside Judea or Israel. Their geography does not remove their Jewish identity.

**Assembly.** An assembly is a gathered community. Related gathering language can serve different purposes in different institutions and texts.

## Additional questions

### Why Greek-speaking Judaism matters

Greek Jewish Scriptures and diaspora communities are not peripheral to Christian origins. The earliest Christian movement spreads through a world where many Jews already live and read Scripture in Greek.

## Deeper reading note

It is risky to project later rabbinic synagogue practice backward unchanged into the first century. Archaeology, inscriptions, literary texts, and later traditions illuminate different times and places.
