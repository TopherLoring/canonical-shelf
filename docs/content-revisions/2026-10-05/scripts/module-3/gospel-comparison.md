# Four Gospels: compare without flattening

Lesson ID: gospel-comparison

**Objective:** Explain how a Gospel’s purpose and arrangement shape its presentation of Jesus.

**Primary reading:** Luke 1:1–4

## Four Gospels: compare without flattening

A Gospel is a written account that proclaims the good news concerning Jesus. It communicates through the way its material is selected and arranged, as well as through individual sayings. Luke’s opening identifies earlier accounts, transmitted testimony, investigation, and an ordered presentation with a stated purpose. That introduction invites us to read the work as a shaped account rather than as a collection of sayings without a setting.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Luke 1:1–4 (BSB).

## Locate the claim in context

The Gospels share their central subject, but their arrangement and emphases differ. Begin by reading a scene within the account that contains it. The writer’s placement can connect that scene with concerns developed around it. A combined retelling may help reconstruct events, yet it can hide those connections and make an author’s distinctive purpose harder to see.

Comparison therefore begins with observation. Record where the accounts agree and where they differ before proposing an explanation. Immediately harmonizing them, or immediately accusing a writer of fraud, skips that first task. A literary observation describes what the accounts do. A historical conclusion about why they differ needs further evidence beyond the difference itself.

## Keep the setting visible

Use Explore to compare Mark’s and Luke’s openings. Note where each begins, what concerns it announces, and how those choices introduce Jesus to a new reader. Then identify a question raised by the comparison. This exercise helps us attend to both accounts instead of making their differences a contest in which one must be discarded. Preserve what each opening contributes before deciding what a combined summary can responsibly say.

## Glossary

**Gospel.** A Gospel is a written account proclaiming the good news concerning Jesus. Its arrangement helps communicate its purpose.

**Arrangement.** Arrangement is the order and placement through which a writer presents material and connects concerns.

**Harmonization.** Harmonization combines accounts into one reconstruction. It can obscure each writer’s distinctive emphasis if comparison is skipped.
