# God’s kingdom and the call to follow

Lesson ID: kingdom-following

**Objective:** Connect Jesus’ announcement of God’s reign with repentance and discipleship.

**Primary reading:** Mark 1:14–20

## God’s kingdom and the call to follow

God’s kingdom refers to God’s reign or active rule, rather than simply a place after death. Mark introduces Jesus’ announcement through fulfillment language and the nearness of this reign. The announcement calls for a response now. Connecting that proclamation with the following calling scenes shows why discipleship involves a changed direction of life as well as learning.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Mark 1:14–20 (BSB).

## Locate the claim in context

Repentance and belief name the response. Repentance involves turning from sin toward a changed direction, not merely feeling badly about oneself. Trust is directed toward the good news. Neither response requires claiming perfect understanding before a beginning is possible.

Jesus’ call to fishers gives the proclamation a narrative expression. He addresses people within their ordinary work, and they begin to follow. Later scenes include misunderstanding and failure, so this first response is not a claim that they instantly become mature. A disciple is a learner and follower; Mark’s continuing narrative shows why both descriptions matter.

## Keep the setting visible

The call’s significance is not identical to a universal instruction that every Christian must leave the same employment. A narrative describes particular people and circumstances, and application requires a reasoned connection. Ask how following Jesus changes priorities, relationships, and conduct in an actual life. That question keeps changed direction central while leaving room for continued learning and different circumstances.

## Reflect on the lesson
**Optional reflection**

What is the difference between feeling regret and changing direction?

**Model response:** Regret names how I feel; changing direction could include admitting harm and altering a repeated behavior. The two can be related without being identical.

## Glossary

**Kingdom of God.** The kingdom of God means God’s reign or active rule, rather than merely a geographical destination.

**Repentance.** Repentance means turning from sin toward a changed direction of life. It involves more than a feeling of regret.

**Disciple.** A disciple is a learner and follower whose understanding develops. An initial response is the beginning of that learning.
