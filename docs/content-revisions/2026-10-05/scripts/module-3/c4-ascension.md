# Ascension: resurrection leads into mission and reign

Lesson ID: c4-ascension

**Objective:** Explain the Ascension as a transition between resurrection appearances, divine reign, Spirit expectation, and the Church’s mission.

**Primary reading:** Acts 1:1–11

## Ascension: resurrection leads into mission and reign

Ascension names Jesus being taken from the disciples’ sight after the resurrection appearances in Luke–Acts. Acts begins by connecting itself with the Gospel narrative and describing those appearances before the departure. The scene is a hinge into the new book: it connects the risen Jesus with divine reign and directs attention toward the Spirit and the community’s mission.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Acts 1:1–11 (BSB).

## Locate the claim in context

The disciples ask about restoring the kingdom to Israel. Jesus does not supply a timetable. The narrative instead directs them toward the promised Spirit and a widening witness beginning in Jerusalem. Witness means testimony, and here that testimony becomes the commission through which Acts’ story will move outward.

The cloud and heavenly imagery belong to biblical language of divine presence and exaltation. Exaltation concerns Jesus’ divine honor and rule. Later Christian theology speaks of Christ’s reign and his session, or place, at God’s right hand. First read Acts 1 as its own narrative transition and commission, so later formulations do not obscure how the scene turns the disciples from questions of timing toward their task.

## Keep the setting visible

The scene prepares for Pentecost by instructing the disciples to wait for the Spirit. The commission is not something they launch on their own timetable or by their own power. Resurrection appearances lead into the promise of witness, the Ascension, and waiting in Jerusalem. Following that movement explains how this scene bridges the Gospel story and the Spirit-empowered mission in Acts.

## Glossary

**Ascension.** The Ascension is Jesus being taken from the disciples’ sight after the resurrection appearances in Luke–Acts.

**Witness.** A witness gives testimony. Acts connects this language with the community’s expanding mission.

**Exaltation.** Exaltation describes Jesus being raised to divine honor and rule. It is part of the theological language surrounding the Ascension.

## Additional questions

### Why the kingdom question matters

Acts opens with an Israel-restoration question and then narrates mission through Jerusalem, Judea/Samaria, and outward. That movement belongs to the book’s theology of mission.

## Deeper reading note

Different New Testament texts express resurrection, exaltation, heavenly session, and Ascension with different narrative and theological emphases. They should be compared rather than forced into one flat chronology without examination.
