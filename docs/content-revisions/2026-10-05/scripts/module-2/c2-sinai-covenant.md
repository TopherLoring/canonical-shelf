# Sinai: a liberated people enters covenant

Lesson ID: c2-sinai-covenant

**Objective:** Connect Sinai covenant with the Exodus that precedes it and distinguish covenant identity from earning liberation.

**Primary reading:** Exodus 19:1–8

## Sinai: a liberated people enters covenant
Israel reaches Sinai as a people already brought out of Egypt. This lesson reads the moment before the commandments and asks how that earlier rescue gives covenant responsibility its setting. God first recalls the deliverance and then describes the relationship into which Israel is called. The sequence explains why the instructions are not the price paid for release from slavery. They describe life within a relationship that has already begun, rather than a technique by which an enslaved people earns its freedom.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Exodus 19:1–8 (BSB).

Notice the order of God’s speech. The image of being carried on eagles’ wings recalls what God has already done; the invitation that follows concerns what Israel is to become. Keep that movement visible as you read the people’s response.

## Treasured possession and priestly vocation
Covenant brings together promise, relationship, identity, and obligation. Exodus 19 describes Israel as a treasured possession and gives the people a priestly vocation and a call to holiness. These terms concern a people’s place and responsibilities within a larger world. A vocation is a calling to a task, so the language should not be converted into a license for moral superiority. The identity named here carries a responsibility rather than establishing that every Israelite action is good.

## What Torah covers
Torah means instruction, and the teaching that follows Sinai addresses several dimensions of shared life. Worship appears alongside violence, property, labor, justice, and care for vulnerable neighbors. These subjects include moral, social, ritual, and legal concerns. Recognizing their variety prevents us from treating Torah as an undifferentiated list whose individual commands can be understood without their place in the community’s covenant life.

## Later debates
Later Christian traditions disagree about how Sinai’s law relates to Gentile Christians. Those debates are important, but they are later questions asked of an earlier body of instruction. Begin with what the laws mean within Israel’s covenant life before tracing their Christian use. Modules 3 and 4 return to the differing interpretations without allowing them to erase Sinai’s own setting.

## What you now know
Sinai follows rescue, and covenant responsibilities describe how the rescued community is to live. Torah gives that life moral, social, ritual, and legal form through instructions about worship, work, justice, and vulnerable people. Israel’s vocation belongs within the wider world rather than licensing superiority. Canonical Shelf understands Christian responsibility through Jesus’ new covenant and its governing law of Love, while retaining earlier covenants as essential context. Other Christian views will be compared later. The next question arises within Exodus itself: what happens when the covenant is broken almost as soon as it is made?

## Glossary

**Sinai.** Sinai is the mountain setting associated with covenant revelation in Exodus. Israel reaches it after deliverance from Egypt.

**Covenant.** A covenant is a binding relationship involving promises, identity, and responsibilities. At Sinai it gives a rescued people a communal vocation.

**Torah.** Torah means instruction. It often refers to the first five biblical books and the moral, social, ritual, and legal teaching they contain.

## Additional questions

### Why the order matters

Exodus → covenant prevents a beginner from imagining that obedience earns the initial liberation. Later Christian grace/law debates should not simply be projected backward onto Sinai.

## Deeper reading note

Biblical covenants are not all identical contracts. The word can describe different forms of relationship, promise, obligation, and divine commitment. Course 5 and Course 6 can examine later covenant frameworks more technically.
