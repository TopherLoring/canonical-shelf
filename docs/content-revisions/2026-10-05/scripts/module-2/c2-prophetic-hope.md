**Objective:** Identify several streams of prophetic hope without collapsing them into one prediction checklist.

## Prophetic hope: king, Spirit, restoration, and peace
Prophetic hope takes several forms within concrete crises. The prophets speak of restored land and community, renewed covenant, justice, divine presence, Spirit, a faithful ruler, purified worship, international peace, and new-creation imagery. These themes do not all function as parts of one prediction chart. This lesson uses Isaiah 11 to identify relationships within that range before examining how later communities combine and interpret them. Their variety matters to understanding the expectations that precede Christian claims about Jesus.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Isaiah 11:1–10 (BSB).

Read Isaiah 11:1–10 and follow its movement from Jesse’s shoot to Spirit-endowed judgment, concern for the vulnerable, and a transformed world. Ask which forms of hope appear together and how the imagery connects them.

## Locate the claim in context
Jesse is David’s father, so the shoot from Jesse evokes royal hope associated with David. Isaiah joins that image to Spirit-endowed judgment, justice for vulnerable people, and cosmic transformation. These are connected images, not a modern timetable for identifying a single event. After the Davidic monarchy’s collapse, royal promises can become future-facing hopes. Other texts emphasize a servant, a prophet, priestly renewal, divine kingship, resurrection, or apocalyptic deliverance. Second Temple Jewish communities combine these materials differently, so messianic expectation cannot be assumed to have one form in every community.

## Keep the setting visible
The later study of Second Temple Judaism first asks what hopes existed before asking how Christians identify Jesus with them. Gospel fulfillment claims must then be considered alongside quotation, allusion, typology, and prophecy as distinct relationships to earlier Scripture. Knowing a passage’s original setting helps explain those connections without treating every later reuse as an identical direct prediction. This preserves the range of prophetic hope while allowing Christian claims to be examined on their own terms.

## Glossary

**Prophetic Hope.** Prophetic hope names future-oriented promises and visions within messages addressing concrete crises. Its forms include restoration, justice, presence, kingship, and peace.

**Jesse.** Jesse is David’s father. The language of a root or shoot of Jesse evokes hope associated with David’s royal line.

**Messianic Expectation.** Messianic expectation is expectation concerning an anointed or divinely appointed figure. Its forms vary across texts and communities rather than constituting one universal checklist.

## Additional questions

### Direct prediction is not the only connection

Later texts can quote, echo, typologically reuse, or reread earlier Scripture. Course 5 teaches those relationships explicitly.

## Deeper reading note

“Messianic prophecy” can become an anachronistic category if every prophetic passage is assumed to function as a direct prediction of Jesus. Christian interpretation can affirm fulfillment while still asking how a passage operated in its earlier literary and historical context.
