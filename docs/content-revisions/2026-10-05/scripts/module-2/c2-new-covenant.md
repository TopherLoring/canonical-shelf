# New covenant: prophetic hope after rupture

Lesson ID: c2-new-covenant

**Objective:** Interpret Jeremiah’s new-covenant promise in its Israel/Judah restoration setting before tracing later Christian use.

**Primary reading:** Jeremiah 31:31–34

## New covenant: prophetic hope after rupture
Jeremiah announces a future new covenant with the house of Israel and the house of Judah in a section concerned with restoration. The promise responds to covenant rupture, exile, and the need for renewed relationship. Beginning with that named audience gives the word new its setting. This lesson follows Jeremiah’s promise before considering the Christian uses that later become prominent in Last Supper traditions and Hebrews. The earlier horizon matters to explaining those uses responsibly.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Jeremiah 31:31–34 (BSB).

Read Jeremiah 31:31–34 and notice who receives the promise. Then follow what changes in the covenant relationship: instruction written on the heart, knowledge of God, and forgiveness. Keep those features together rather than letting the word new alone determine the interpretation.

## Locate the claim in context
Instruction written on the heart depicts the teaching becoming internal to the people’s life. Knowledge of God and forgiveness likewise concern renewed relationship after rupture. The passage names Israel and Judah as recipients, so its newness does not mean that Jewish people or their Scriptures become disposable. Later Christian texts apply new-covenant language to Jesus, especially in Last Supper traditions and Hebrews. That important Christian interpretation should be explained as later reception while preserving what Jeremiah’s own promise says and to whom it is addressed.

## Keep the setting visible
Supersessionism can name the claim that the Church replaces Israel in a way that makes Jewish covenantal identity obsolete; its forms and definitions vary. Distinguishing Jeremiah’s setting from later Christian use guards against simply erasing that setting. Canonical Shelf presents Christian fulfillment claims while respecting the continuing Jewish context of inherited texts. The promise of renewed relationship remains the starting point, and Jewish interpretation does not accept the Christian identification of Jesus as its fulfillment. These differences need to be explained rather than hidden beneath a shared phrase.

## Glossary

**New Covenant.** The new covenant is Jeremiah’s promise of renewed relationship with Israel and Judah. Later Christian interpretation gives the promise an important place in its understanding of Jesus.

**Internalized Instruction.** Internalized instruction names Jeremiah’s image of divine teaching written on the heart. It concerns the place of instruction within renewed covenant life.

**Supersessionism.** Supersessionism can name the claim that the Church replaces Israel in a way that renders Jewish covenantal identity obsolete. Its forms and definitions vary, so particular claims need careful explanation.
