# Hear the prophets in their own setting

Lesson ID: prophets

**Objective:** Relate prophetic speech to worship, justice, crisis, and hope before using it as a prediction or slogan.

**Primary reading:** Amos 5:21–24

## Hear the prophets in their own setting
A prophet communicates a message understood as coming from God. Biblical prophetic messages address concrete communal situations through warning, judgment, instruction, symbolic action, and hope. Prediction belongs to some of this writing, but reducing prophecy to a code for distant events can conceal its confrontation with present wrongdoing. This lesson relates prophetic speech to worship, justice, crisis, and hope by beginning with the audience and concern of each message.

## Read the passage with the question in view
> **Read:** Amos 5:21–24 (BSB).

Read Amos 5:21–24 with attention to the contrast between rejected worship and the demand for justice. Ask what that contrast tells us about the community being addressed before turning a line into a present slogan.

## Locate the claim in context
Amos challenges religious activity disconnected from communal conduct. The rejection of worship has force because the people’s treatment of neighbors does not accord with their religious expression; it does not establish that all worship is worthless. Isaiah likewise connects a call for change with defending vulnerable people. Micah rejects the premise that larger offerings can replace justice, mercy, and humble life with God. These messages put ethical responsibility at the center of religious practice. Their different arguments must be followed rather than reduced to interchangeable slogans.

## Keep the setting visible
Jeremiah’s letter addresses displaced people and calls for ordinary life and concern for the place where they live. Its hope belongs within that communal address rather than automatically guaranteeing immediate career success, wealth, or freedom from hardship to an individual. A responsible prophetic reading asks who is addressed, what conduct is challenged, what hope is offered, and how the language functions. Christians relate prophetic hope to Jesus in several ways; explain those relationships while preserving earlier settings and Jewish interpretation. Present application likewise needs a reasoned connection and evidence, rather than attaching a prophet’s authority to a preferred cause.

## Glossary

**Prophet.** A prophet communicates a message understood as coming from God. Biblical prophetic speech can include warning, judgment, instruction, symbolic action, and hope.

**Justice.** Justice is right dealing that attends to relationships, institutions, and vulnerable people. Prophetic criticism connects it with the conduct of worshiping communities.

**Oracle.** An oracle is a prophetic declaration or message. Its meaning should be read within its audience, setting, and argument.

## Deeper reading note

Compare a warning with a promise in the same prophetic book. How do they relate? Do not assume that one vivid line supplies a complete policy for a different setting.
