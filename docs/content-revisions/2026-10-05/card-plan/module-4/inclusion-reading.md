## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 8 · 622 / 637

The guide explicitly affirms LGBTQ dignity, relationships, marriage, and equal participation in ministry. It does not claim that every Christian tradition shares its conclusion. Non-affirming readers often appeal to the continuing scope of prohibitions. Affirming readers examine ancient contexts, the relationships being described, and the wider ethical witness. Scope means the range of people or conduct to which a claim applies; disagreement about it requires an argument rather than an assumption.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 8 · 601 / 637

Discussion can represent disagreement without permitting harassment or requiring LGBTQ participants to justify their worth. Analytical rigor concerns claims and evidence. Interpretation also has consequences for communal life, so stated welcome must be examined in practice. Does it include relationships, leadership, and equal participation, or does general language leave those questions unanswered? The connection between interpretation and conduct returns us to the objective: transparent reasoning should explain a community's commitments and their effects while preserving participants' dignity.
