## Atonement: several images of one saving work

### 1 of 9 · 519 / 637

Atonement names Christ's saving work in addressing sin and reconciliation with God. Reconciliation means restoring a relationship across estrangement. In 2 Corinthians, Paul attributes that restoration to God's initiative through Christ. The direction of the action matters: humanity is not presented as first making itself worthy and then persuading God to act. Our objective is to distinguish several images of this saving work without reducing the cross to an example of kindness that people could reproduce unaided.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 9 · 588 / 637

Paul connects Christ with sin and righteousness and those reconciled through him. Christians draw on this passage and others to explain representation, substitution, sacrifice, and exchange. Each explanation gathers evidence and reasons about it. A theological model therefore is not identical to a single metaphor or to every statement in the passage.

Penal substitution describes Christ as bearing the penalty due to sinners. Victory accounts emphasize the defeat of sin and death, while reconciliation emphasizes a restored relationship.

The guide accommodates penal substitution but does not make it the sole scriptural explanation of how salvation is accomplished. Comparing an image's emphasis with another image's emphasis is more informative than assuming that different words must describe mutually exclusive events.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 9 · 537 / 637

Christ's necessary sacrifice and resurrection remain central to this account. The present lesson asks what Christ accomplishes; receiving grace asks how people receive the gift. Distinguishing those questions keeps an explanatory model from turning into a transaction diagram that exhausts salvation, and keeps moral example from replacing saving significance. Compare the reconciliation language here with the victory language in Colossians 2:13–15. Explain what each illuminates and where a larger theological argument is still needed.
