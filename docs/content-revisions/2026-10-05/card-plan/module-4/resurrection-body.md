## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 8 · 577 / 637

Paul contrasts perishable and imperishable, weakness and power, natural and spiritual. Imperishable means not subject to the decay described in the contrast. The phrase spiritual body should not automatically be made to mean no body at all. Within the historic teaching used here, the concern is transformed embodied life, so an explanation must retain embodiment as well as change.

This hope does not make present bodies disposable. Claiming that God overcomes death differs from claiming that physical existence never mattered.
