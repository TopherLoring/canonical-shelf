## Complete an independent reading project

### 1 of 9 · 512 / 637

An introductory guide should eventually help you read with less dependence on the guide. Begin an independent project with a manageable whole book such as Ruth, Jonah, Philippians, Philemon, or Jude. Read the complete book in Explore before concentrating on a few verses. Record an initial question without deciding its answer in advance. Our objective is to build a contextual reading whose evidence, interpretation, disagreement, and application can be explained and reviewed, including what remains uncertain.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 9 · 630 / 637

Build an evidence record by identifying the kind of writing, the audience where known, repeated concerns, important turns, and details that complicate your first impression. Separate the text's explicit statements from historical reconstructions and personal associations. A reconstruction infers missing circumstances from evidence; it should not be presented as if the text directly supplied them. Investigate a factual claim with a reliable source when it matters to the argument.

Next explain the passage's meaning in context and compare at least one alternative interpretation.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 9 · 544 / 637

Finally propose a concrete application and consider its effects on other people. The goal is not to make Scripture confirm every first preference, but to develop an accountable reading that can be revised. Return to the text, the people affected, and the wider Christian conversation when evidence or concerns change. An application remains open to examination even when its intentions are good.

The project is complete when you can explain the reasoning, not when every question has disappeared.
