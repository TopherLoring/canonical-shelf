## Stay honest with difficult questions

### 1 of 9 · 619 / 637

Questions about evil and suffering are intellectual and personal at the same time. Luke 13 challenges the inference that victims suffered because they were worse sinners than others. The guide distinguishes wrongdoing enabled by human freedom from disease and disasters within a dynamic natural world, while acknowledging that this does not explain why God permits every loss. Our objective is to address suffering, difficult ethical texts, and religious difference through evidence and care. Reading directly and admitting what remains unresolved protects inquiry from adding unsupported accusations to someone's pain.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 9 · 585 / 637

God can bring healing and good from suffering without making the suffering good in itself or every tragedy a deliberate lesson. Lament, uncertainty, practical help, and hope can therefore belong together. Biblical miracles deserve serious study alongside differing interpretations, but neither a diagnosis nor an unhealed condition establishes possession or inadequate faith. An account of God's action cannot simply be assumed from the fact of suffering.

Conquest, slavery, and restrictions on women likewise require direct examination.
