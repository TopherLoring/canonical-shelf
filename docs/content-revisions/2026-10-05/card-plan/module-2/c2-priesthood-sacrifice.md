## Priesthood and sacrifice: worship has roles and purposes

### 1 of 8 · 541 / 637

Leviticus begins within the tabernacle world established by Exodus. This lesson moves from the place of worship to the people and actions involved in it. Priests, offerings, purity practices, sacred times, and communal responsibilities give worship a structure. Sacrifice is therefore a category containing distinct actions with different purposes, rather than one mechanism repeated without variation. Understanding these distinctions also helps explain why later Christian writers can use priestly and sacrificial language in several ways.

## Different offerings, different purposes

### 3 of 8 · 500 / 637

An offering is a gift or sacrifice presented in worship. Different offerings can emphasize gift, dedication, fellowship, purification, reparation, or atonement. These functions concern different situations, so one cannot infer the purpose of every offering merely from the general word sacrifice. Blood has an important place in several rites, but that does not make every rite identical. First learn what each action does in its own ritual setting before fitting it into a later theological account.

## Priests, households, and ordinary ethics

### 5 of 8 · 475 / 637

Priests have particular ritual responsibilities on behalf of the community. They act within a wider worshiping life that also involves households, individual worshipers, Levites, sacred space, and ordinary ethics. Separating roles helps us understand the system; separating ritual from how people treat one another would obscure it. Worship and ethical conduct both belong within Israel’s response to God, so ritual cannot be understood as the only part of life that matters.

## Later Christian language about sacrifice

### 7 of 8 · 349 / 637

Later Christian texts apply priestly and sacrificial language to Jesus in multiple ways. The underlying vocabulary becomes clearer when Israel’s worship is studied first. Without those distinctions, sacrifice can become only a vague way of saying that something costly happened, leaving unexplained the particular ritual imagery a writer has chosen.

## What you now know

### 8 of 8 · 585 / 637

Israel’s worship brings priests, offerings, purity, sacred times, and everyday ethics into a structured relationship. The offerings have different purposes, including gift, fellowship, purification, and reparation. Scholars debate precise terminology and the meaning of particular blood rites, and Christians differ over how this language applies to Jesus. Knowing the Levitical system provides a basis for comparing those readings without flattening ritual diversity. The next lesson focuses on an annual set of rites: what happens when the high priest enters the innermost sanctuary?
