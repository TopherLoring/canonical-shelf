## Hebrew poetry: lines speak to each other

### 1 of 8 · 543 / 637

Israel’s prayers, songs, sayings, and arguments ask how people can live and speak to God amid uneven circumstances. This unit begins with reading Hebrew poetry because its line relationships shape those questions. Parallelism is a broad term for meaningful relationships between poetic lines. Repetition, development, contrast, intensification, imagery, sound, and structure can all contribute to meaning. No rigid category accounts for every pair. Reading the lines together therefore gives us more than reading each as an isolated statement.

## The heavens speak without words

### 3 of 8 · 370 / 637

Psalm 19 describes heavens, sky, day, and night communicating without ordinary speech. Its cosmic imagery gives form to a theological claim. Reading that language as poetry asks how the image communicates its meaning; it does not make the claim meaningless. Asking whether the sky literally has vocal cords would miss the kind of language through which the psalm speaks.

## What the second line does

### 4 of 8 · 394 / 637

The next line may restate a claim, sharpen it, extend it, contrast with it, or shift its perspective. Even repetition can change emphasis through a different image or phrasing. Ask what has changed instead of deleting the second line as unnecessary. That attention helps explain how the paired lines produce meaning together rather than supplying interchangeable versions of one prose sentence.

## What you now know

### 8 of 8 · 536 / 637

Hebrew poetry often makes meaning through relationships between lines. A second line may repeat, sharpen, extend, contrast, or shift what came before, while imagery gives the claim a form that isolated prose cannot reproduce. Scholars describe parallelism in different ways, and no single classification fits every case. The shared reading practice is to ask what changes and why it matters. Apply it beyond the Psalms to prophets, wisdom, songs, and laments. Carry forward the question of what this poetry enables people to say to God.
