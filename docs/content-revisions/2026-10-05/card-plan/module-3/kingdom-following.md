## God’s kingdom and the call to follow

### 1 of 8 · 375 / 637

God’s kingdom refers to God’s reign or active rule, rather than simply a place after death. Mark introduces Jesus’ announcement through fulfillment language and the nearness of this reign. The announcement calls for a response now. Connecting that proclamation with the following calling scenes shows why discipleship involves a changed direction of life as well as learning.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 8 · 590 / 637

Repentance and belief name the response. Repentance involves turning from sin toward a changed direction, not merely feeling badly about oneself. Trust is directed toward the good news. Neither response requires claiming perfect understanding before a beginning is possible.

Jesus’ call to fishers gives the proclamation a narrative expression. He addresses people within their ordinary work, and they begin to follow. Later scenes include misunderstanding and failure, so this first response is not a claim that they instantly become mature.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 8 · 434 / 637

The call’s significance is not identical to a universal instruction that every Christian must leave the same employment. A narrative describes particular people and circumstances, and application requires a reasoned connection. Ask how following Jesus changes priorities, relationships, and conduct in an actual life. That question keeps changed direction central while leaving room for continued learning and different circumstances.
