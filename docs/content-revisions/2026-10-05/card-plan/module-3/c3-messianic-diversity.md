## Messiah was not one single expectation

### 1 of 7 · 359 / 637

Messiah means anointed one. In Israel’s Scriptures, anointing can mark kings, priests, and other appointed figures. Davidic royal promises become an especially important source of later hope, but the title does not already carry one complete Christian definition before the Gospels. The lesson’s aim is to understand the range of expectation behind the title.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 7 · 549 / 637

Second Temple texts describe a range of hopes: a Davidic ruler, priestly figures, prophetic restoration, heavenly or apocalyptic agents, national deliverance, renewed Temple life, and resurrection. Some expectations do not center on a messianic figure at all. Texts combine these possibilities differently, so we should not turn them into one checklist accepted by every Jewish community.

Psalm 2 provides royal language that Christians later reuse. Its earlier work concerns kingship and divine rule.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 7 · 413 / 637

This range of expectation explains the weight of Gospel questions about Jesus’ identity. Calling him Messiah does not simply match him to a universally agreed job description. It places a claim about him within several possible scriptural and interpretive expectations. As we enter the Gospels, ask which aspect of that inherited hope a particular narrative develops and what it claims is distinctive about Jesus.
