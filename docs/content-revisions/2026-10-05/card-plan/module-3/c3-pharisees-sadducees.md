## Pharisees and Sadducees: disagreements inside Judaism

### 1 of 7 · 403 / 637

Pharisees and Sadducees are distinct Jewish groups within the late Second Temple world. Neither name stands for all Judaism. Their differences involve social location, authority, interpretation, resurrection, and other questions. Because the sources describing them have their own perspectives, the task is to identify particular disagreements without turning either group into a fixed personality type.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 7 · 595 / 637

Acts 23 identifies a clear disagreement: Pharisees affirm resurrection, while Sadducees deny it. That observation is not a complete description of either group. It does establish that resurrection is already a debated Jewish question. Christianity therefore enters an existing discussion rather than introducing the subject into a uniform Jewish belief system.

The Gospels frequently portray conflict between Jesus and Pharisees over Sabbath, purity, interpretation, and practice. Such arguments take place within a shared Jewish scriptural world.

Conflict can reflect proximity because people who share commitments still disagree over their meaning. Later Christian rhetoric has often turned these disputes into caricatures of Jews. Treating Pharisee as a synonym for hypocrite repeats that distortion instead of explaining the historical group.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 7 · 486 / 637

Our sources associate Sadducees especially with elite, priestly, and Temple settings, but the available evidence is limited. The Temple’s destruction in 70 CE changes the conditions under which these groups exist; their identities disappear or transform. Later rabbinic Judaism should not simply be equated with the Pharisees without qualification. Carry forward specific claims with their evidence and limits, rather than using a group name as an explanation for every Gospel conflict.
