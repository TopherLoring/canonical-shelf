## Practice belonging, prayer, and care

### 1 of 9 · 494 / 637

Christian practices give embodied and communal expression to faith. Baptism, the rite of water, connects belonging with Christ’s death and resurrection. Communities also promise support for the person baptized. Canonical Shelf teaches this spiritual and social significance without declaring baptism necessary for salvation. Infant and believer’s baptism should both be described fairly. The larger question is how practices express Christian life without becoming achievements that earn grace.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 9 · 606 / 637

Communion remembers Christ’s sacrifice, expresses communion with him, and gathers people in unity. This guide affirms an open table: all are welcome, always, without membership, baptism, or doctrinal agreement as conditions of admission. Other churches have different practices. Describing those practices accurately allows comparison while keeping this guide’s welcome explicit.

Christians also differ over Christ’s presence in Communion. Real-presence accounts, spiritual-presence accounts, and memorial emphases require explanation rather than caricature.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 9 · 528 / 637

Prayer includes requests, gratitude, lament, confession, and listening. Jesus challenges prayer performed for admiration and offers a pattern of dependence, forgiveness, and concern for God’s purposes. The practice does not guarantee a requested outcome, replace medical care, or justify blaming illness on insufficient faith. Its meaning must remain distinct from claims that successful performance can purchase a result.

First John relates love among people to God’s prior love.

### 6 of 9 · 442 / 637

Formation means developing the habits and understanding through which that relationship takes practical form: speech, service, ordinary conduct, and repair after harm. A brief prayer, attentive reading, or concrete act of care can provide a sustainable practice. These are responses to grace, not accumulated religious achievements that purchase salvation. The practices belong together because they connect belonging, relationship, and care.
