## Parables: hearing the question behind the story

### 1 of 8 · 365 / 637

A parable is a story or comparison that invites insight through a familiar situation. In Luke 15, the situation around Jesus supplies the question the stories address. People approach him, and others complain about his welcome and shared meals. Reading a sheep or coin by itself can miss that conversation. Begin with the complaint, then follow the stories’ answer.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 8 · 575 / 637

Both stories move from a search to finding and celebration. The repeated movement makes recovery a reason for joy. That pattern helps us ask how the stories challenge resentment of Jesus’ welcome. It also gives a stronger basis for interpretation than assigning a separate hidden doctrine to each incidental object.

The stories engage imagination as well as direct instruction. A reader may identify with someone responsibly keeping what matters, then have to reconsider the response to recovering someone previously dismissed.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 8 · 451 / 637

The course’s practice of welcome can draw on this challenge, while distinguishing Luke’s setting from every modern admission debate. Ask whether a community celebrates restoration or resents the attention given to people beyond its favored circle. This question connects present practice with the stories’ concern. A responsible application also states its limits instead of assuming that every contemporary disagreement is already the same situation.
