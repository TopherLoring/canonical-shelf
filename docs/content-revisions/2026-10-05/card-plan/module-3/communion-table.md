## Communion: remembering Christ at a shared table

### 1 of 8 · 342 / 637

Communion is the Christian shared meal also called the Eucharist or the Lord’s Supper. Paul links it with Jesus’ body and the new covenant in his blood. Remembrance and proclamation of Christ’s death give the practice its focus. It is therefore more than a generic social meal, but the way people behave together still belongs to its meaning.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 8 · 633 / 637

Paul’s surrounding argument describes division and unequal treatment within the gathering. His later instruction to wait for one another connects the account of the ritual with that problem. If examination is read only as private introspection, the harm done to other participants can disappear from view. Reading the whole argument restores the connection between what the meal proclaims and how the community conducts it.

Christians differ in their accounts of Christ’s presence. Bodily real-presence accounts, spiritual-presence accounts, and memorial emphases make distinct claims.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 8 · 478 / 637

Canonical Shelf affirms an open table: all are welcome, always, without membership, baptism, or doctrinal agreement as admission requirements. Other churches set different requirements, which should be explained clearly. Stating this guide’s commitment does not make all traditions agree or remove the need to interpret Paul carefully. The shared-meal setting challenges us to consider whether practical arrangements support welcome or instead produce humiliation and exclusion.
