## Acts 15: a community works through disagreement

### 1 of 8 · 417 / 637

A Gentile is someone who is not Jewish. Acts 15 concerns the requirements placed on Gentile believers and shows a community working through a serious disagreement. Inherited practice and widening belonging raise questions that cannot be resolved merely by pretending conflict is absent. The account presents a shared process of discernment, or responsible judgment, through which the disputed requirement is examined.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 8 · 585 / 637

Follow the different kinds of reason the narrative presents: debate, testimony about God’s work, and an appeal to Scripture. Identify each before explaining how they contribute together to the proposal. The account does not merely announce an answer. Its movement connects a disputed question with reasons and a response directed to that question.

That response has a specific community and scope. It is not a blanket authorization for every later proposal, and it does not make every contemporary disagreement identical to this dispute.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 8 · 445 / 637

For a contemporary discussion, begin by stating the actual question and identifying who is affected. Hear the evidence, describe opposing concerns fairly, and explain the proposed decision’s scope. These practices draw on the account’s reasoned movement without assuming the historical situations are the same. A community can learn through disagreement, but its process should remain attentive to the people whose belonging is under discussion.
