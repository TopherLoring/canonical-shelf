## Passover and the passion: an old liberation story enters a new claim

### 1 of 7 · 435 / 637

Passover remembers the Exodus and liberation within Jewish worship. The passion traditions occur in that festival world, and the Synoptic Gospels frame Jesus’ final meal with the disciples in relation to Passover. Bringing the earlier Exodus study into the scene makes the meal’s inherited meaning visible. Christian interpretation adds a claim about Jesus within this history rather than making Passover an unexplained Christian term.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 7 · 591 / 637

Jesus interprets the bread and cup through his body and covenant blood. Luke also uses new-covenant language. That wording connects the meal with prophetic restoration, including Jeremiah 31, as well as with sacrifice and covenant imagery. These relationships help explain why the meal gathers several strands of Scripture rather than relying on a single isolated reference.

The Gospels and Paul preserve related but differing meal traditions. Comparing their wording and framing matters because a combined account can obscure the differences.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 7 · 468 / 637

Christians interpret Jesus’ death through Passover, covenant, sacrifice, and liberation patterns. This is a Christian interpretive claim. It does not cancel Passover’s continuing Jewish meaning or practice. Keep the Exodus and Jeremiah backgrounds visible alongside the later claim, and preserve the Gospel differences rather than forcing them into an identical chronology. The next lesson asks how remembering Christ shapes the treatment of those at the shared table.
