## Resistance, revolt, and “Zealot” language

### 1 of 7 · 411 / 637

Responses to Roman rule range from accommodation and negotiation to protest, tax resistance, prophetic movements, banditry, and armed revolt. They occur at different times and do not form one program. Ancient accounts portray motives that combine politics, religious commitments, local grievances, and hopes for divine intervention. To understand these movements, we need labels that preserve their differences.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 7 · 555 / 637

Zealot is often used loosely for any Jewish opponent of Rome. In a more specific historical sense, the label is especially associated with a movement during the revolt beginning in 66 CE. Zeal and resistance existed earlier, but that does not put every earlier activist into the later movement. Chronology matters when a name implies an organized group.

Acts 5 recalls Theudas and Judas the Galilean as leaders of failed movements. Josephus also discusses Judas and resistance connected with Roman taxation.
