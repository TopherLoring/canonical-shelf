Acts begins by connecting itself with the Gospel narrative and describing those appearances before the departure. The scene is a hinge into the new book: it connects the risen Jesus with divine reign and directs attention toward the Spirit and the community’s mission.

## Locate the claim in context

### 3 of 6 · 607 / 637

The disciples ask about restoring the kingdom to Israel. Jesus does not supply a timetable. The narrative instead directs them toward the promised Spirit and a widening witness beginning in Jerusalem. Witness means testimony, and here that testimony becomes the commission through which Acts’ story will move outward.

The cloud and heavenly imagery belong to biblical language of divine presence and exaltation. Exaltation concerns Jesus’ divine honor and rule. Later Christian theology speaks of Christ’s reign and his session, or place, at God’s right hand.

## Keep the setting visible

### 5 of 6 · 390 / 637

The scene prepares for Pentecost by instructing the disciples to wait for the Spirit. The commission is not something they launch on their own timetable or by their own power. Resurrection appearances lead into the promise of witness, the Ascension, and waiting in Jerusalem. Following that movement explains how this scene bridges the Gospel story and the Spirit-empowered mission in Acts.
