Genesis 1

Berean Standard Bible

The Creation

1In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. 2Now the earth was formless and void, and darkness was over the surface of the deep. And the Spirit of God was hovering over the surface of the waters. 3And God said, “Let there be light,” and there was light. 4And God saw that the light was good, and He separated the light from the darkness. 5God called the light “day,” and the darkness He called “night.” And there was evening, and there was morning—the first day.

The Second Day

6And God said, “Let there be an expanse between the waters, to separate the waters from the waters.” 7So God made the expanse and separated the waters beneath it from the waters above. And it was so. 8God called the expanse “sky.” And there was evening, and there was morning—the second day.

Theologian

You’re reading Genesis 1:2
What does “the Spirit of God was hovering” mean here?

The Hebrew word here, ruach, can mean “spirit,” “wind,” or “breath,” so translations differ: the BSB reads “the Spirit of God,” while some, like the NRSV, read “a wind from God.”

The verb “hovering” appears again in Deuteronomy 32:11, of an eagle hovering over its young, which suggests watchful care rather than motion alone.

How it has been read
  • Many Jewish interpreters read it as God’s presence, or a divine wind, over the waters.
  • Christian tradition commonly reads it as the Holy Spirit, active in creation.

The text itself doesn’t settle which reading is right.

  • Deuteronomy 32:11, Law, open in the reader
  • Genesis 1:2, Law, open in the reader
