# Canonical Shelf — Multi-Course Curriculum Redesign

## Objective

Reorganize Canonical Shelf from one overloaded scored course into a progressive six-course curriculum while preserving the strongest existing authored material, stable existing activity IDs, local-first learner state, Bible/Topics/Practice ownership boundaries, and the product's interactive learning identity.

The curriculum must remain rigorous but low-friction: concise multi-scene lessons, game-style active checks throughout every lesson, unit-level mastery, spaced retrieval, cross-course transfer, progressive-disclosure drawers, and first-class glossaries. Essential understanding stays in the core scene sequence; drawers, Topics, Bible, and Advanced Study add background, nuance, evidence, and scholarly depth without becoming prerequisites for basic comprehension.

## Latest owner decisions superseding the prior baseline

The previous 25-unit / 70-lesson / 69-mastery / 139-activity structure remains the migration baseline, not the target curriculum ceiling. The owner explicitly approved replacing the single-course structure with the six-course model below. Existing stable IDs should survive where content is retained; new lessons and mastery activities receive new stable IDs. Learner-state migration must preserve completed legacy work and map it into the new hierarchy.

No maximum lesson count per unit is permitted. Unit and lesson counts follow subject need.

## Program architecture

### Course 1 — Bible & Christianity: Foundations
A genuine adult-beginner survey course that introduces the whole map before specialization.

1. Christianity in One View
2. What the Bible Is
3. How We Got the Bible
4. How to Read It
5. Theology You Need Before Continuing
6. Christian Practice
7. Christians Do Not All Read the Same Way
8. The Biblical Story in One View
9. Foundations Synthesis

Course 1 briefly but substantively previews the material later developed in Courses 2–6. A learner who stops after Course 1 should possess real introductory biblical literacy rather than a glossary of names.

### Course 2 — Israel: Exodus, Covenant, Temple & Prophetic Hope
The missing historical-biblical bridge.

1. Egypt and Exodus
2. Sinai and Covenant
3. Tabernacle, Ark and Presence
4. Sacrifice, Holiness and Sacred Time
5. Land, Judges and Kings
6. Temple and Kingdom
7. Prophets, Exile and Destruction
8. Restoration and Prophetic Hope

Required new coverage includes Passover, plagues/sea crossing, Sinai covenant, golden calf, tabernacle, Ark, priesthood, sacrifice, Day of Atonement, clean/unclean, sacred calendar, Davidic covenant, temple development, exile, restoration, and prophetic/messianic hope.

### Course 3 — From Exile to Jesus: The Second Temple World
Bridges Persian-period restoration to the Gospel world.

1. After the Exile
2. The Greek World
3. Hasmoneans, Rome and Herod
4. Jewish Life and Jewish Diversity
5. Hope and Expectation
6. Entering the Gospels

Coverage includes Hellenization, Maccabean crisis, Hasmoneans, Roman rule, Herod, Temple/synagogue/diaspora, Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes/Qumran, revolutionary currents, resurrection debates, apocalyptic expectation, messianic diversity, and the first-century Jewish context of Jesus.

### Course 4 — Jesus, the Gospels & the Early Church

1. Four Gospels
2. Jesus and the Kingdom
3. Jesus' Teaching
4. Jesus and Israel's Story
5. Passion and Resurrection
6. Pentecost and the Jerusalem Church
7. Paul and Gentile Inclusion
8. Christianity Begins to Expand

This course reuses and deepens strong existing Gospel, cross/resurrection, Acts, Paul, Communion, prayer, and community material while adding Sermon on the Mount breadth, Ascension, Pentecost, and stronger historical continuity with Courses 2–3.

### Course 5 — Advanced Biblical Interpretation

1. Text, Manuscripts and Transmission
2. Translation
3. Genre
4. Scripture Interpreting Scripture
5. Gospel and Letter Study
6. Building an Interpretation

Course 1 teaches the essentials; Course 5 develops specialist competence: textual criticism, equivalence theory, Hebrew poetry mechanics, Synoptic relationships, ancient authorship/composition, quotation/allusion/typology, prophecy/fulfillment, argument mapping, and independent interpretation.

### Course 6 — Theology, Traditions & Difficult Questions

1. God and Christ
2. Humanity, Sin and Salvation
3. Providence and Christian Life
4. Church and Practice
5. Christian Traditions
6. Difficult Texts and Questions
7. Resurrection and Final Hope

Course 1 introduces essential theology and practice early. Course 6 provides the deeper comparative and contested material already authored across the current doctrine, practice, traditions, difficult-questions, and hope units.

## Lesson experience contract

A lesson is a multi-scene experience, not one long card and not a lecture page. Scene count varies by subject need.

Typical roles:

1. Orient
2. Read / inspect evidence
3. Explain
4. Visualize
5. Compare / contextualize
6. Interact
7. Feedback
8. Connect
9. Reflect / apply when appropriate
10. Continue

Every substantive lesson must contain active learning before completion. Multiple checks are preferred when they test distinct objectives. Conventional multiple choice is permitted only when it is the best interaction, not as the default.

Supported interaction families include sequencing, matching, shelf placement, map placement, timeline reconstruction, diagram assembly, sorting, evidence classification, context reconstruction, verse rebuilding, comparison, scenario reasoning, argument mapping, interpretation distinctions, synthesis, and capstone transfer. Drag interactions require keyboard/non-drag equivalents.

## Progressive-disclosure drawers

Any scene may expose optional drawers such as:

- Background
- Why this matters
- Go deeper
- Text & translation
- Compare interpretations
- People & places
- Related passages
- How we know
- Common questions
- Original languages
- Later reception
- Sources / bibliography

Drawers enrich the lesson but must not contain information required to understand or pass the core lesson unless explicitly marked as a required scene. Opening drawers must not reset scene state or lesson progress.

## Glossary contract

Glossary support is first-class and layered:

- inline quick definition;
- lesson glossary drawer;
- course glossary;
- global glossary;
- advanced lexical note where useful.

A glossary entry may include core definition, expanded explanation, original-language term when useful, related passages, related concepts, common misunderstanding, course/lesson appearances, Topic links, and Advanced Study links. Glossary terms feed Practice and spaced retrieval.

## Assessment and retention contract

### Lesson check
Immediate evidence that the learner understood the current concept.

### Unit mastery
Integrates several lessons into a richer task. Every scored unit receives authored mastery unless a unit is explicitly non-scored.

### Course synthesis
Each course ends with a cumulative synthesis/capstone.

### Spaced retrieval
Important knowledge reappears on the 1 → 3 → 7 → 14 → 30 → 60 day schedule unless evidence supports a better interval. Reviews should change form over time rather than merely repeat the original question.

### Cross-course transfer
Prior knowledge should reappear later in changed contexts: Passover in the Last Supper; Sinai/covenant in Paul; Temple/presence in John/Hebrews/Revelation; Second Temple groups in Gospel disputes; prophecy/typology in Course 5.

Progress states must distinguish completion, review-due, retained, improving, and mastered. Skipping does not imply mastery; Practice does not count as course completion.

## Content-depth rule

Canonical Shelf teaches casually but does not teach shallowly. Core scenes must provide enough historical setting, textual evidence, vocabulary, relationships, interpretive boundaries, and reasoning for genuine understanding. Optional layers add breadth, nuance, evidence, alternate readings, scholarship, and specialized detail rather than repairing an inadequate core explanation.

## Existing-content preservation strategy

The current 70 authored guided lessons and 69 mastery activities are migration assets, not disposable material. Reuse strong lessons where they fit the new objectives. Preserve stable IDs for retained lessons and mastery activities even when their course/unit placement changes. Add new content only where the audited curriculum materially lacks prerequisite knowledge or necessary synthesis.

Current strengths to preserve include textual-variant/translation work, contextual interpretation, Gospel comparison, Acts 15 discernment, atonement models, baptism, Communion, prayer, doctrine/traditions/difficult questions, apocalypse/new creation, and independent-study reasoning.

## Learner-state migration

- Existing `lesson:<id>` and `mastery:<id>` activity IDs remain stable.
- New activities receive immutable IDs.
- Program/course/unit membership is metadata, not part of the stable legacy ID.
- Existing completion/review/mastery evidence is retained when an activity moves.
- New unit/course mastery is not inferred merely because legacy component lessons were complete; it may be earned through a synthesis challenge.
- Export/import schema changes must be versioned and deterministic.

## UX/product outcomes

- Course home becomes a six-course roadmap with three meaningful achievement levels: Foundations Complete; Biblical Literacy Core Complete (Courses 1–4); Advanced Canonical Shelf Complete (Courses 1–6).
- Course 1 gives the complete mental map and explicit optional links into later courses.
- Course lesson scenes remain viewport-aware and interaction-rich rather than long scrolling documents.
- Unit/course progress visualizes completion, mastery and retention separately.
- Topics remain reference, not scored curriculum.
- Practice remains reinforcement, not a second curriculum.
- Bible owns shelf/browse/reader.
- Advanced Study may expose full scholarly treatment without burdening the beginner flow.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Six courses are represented in the generated catalog with stable course IDs and ordered units.
2. Existing activity IDs remain stable and existing learner-state imports continue to resolve.
3. Course 1 includes introductory material from historical story, interpretation, textual transmission/translation, theology, practice, traditions, and difficult-question methodology.
4. Course 2 contains the full Exodus → covenant → tabernacle/Ark → sacrifice/priesthood → temple → prophets → exile → restoration/hope bridge.
5. Course 3 contains sufficient Second Temple history and Jewish diversity to make Course 4 intelligible without assuming prior specialist knowledge.
6. Every scored lesson has at least one meaningful active check; every scored unit has a mastery/synthesis path; every course has a capstone.
7. Lesson data supports ordered scenes, optional drawers and glossary references without requiring DOM inference.
8. Glossary supports inline, lesson, course and global lookup and can feed Practice.
9. Spaced-review metadata supports 1/3/7/14/30/60-day retrieval and cross-course transfer.
10. Topics remain outside completion; Practice remains reinforcement.
11. Keyboard, touch, screen-reader, reduced-motion and non-drag equivalents remain supported.
12. Automated validation checks graph/schema integrity, stable IDs, required interaction coverage, unit/course mastery, cross-links, content references, and learner-state migration.
13. Human review gates include editorial/theological accuracy, adult-beginner clarity, interaction quality, responsive composition, accessibility, and novice usability.

## Risks and rollback

Primary risks are learner-state loss, content duplication, curriculum bloat, shallow survey coverage, weakened game-style learning, theological/editorial drift, and runtime regressions caused by assumptions about the old fixed counts. Mitigation is stable-ID preservation, explicit contracts, staged migration, schema-driven count validation, content-body auditing before replacement, and human review of representative golden lessons in each course.

Rollback anchor is the branch point from `main`. The redesign ships behind the new catalog/schema and must not destroy or rewrite legacy progress records during migration.
